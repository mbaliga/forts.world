## Documented
- Ksar on a hill on the east/left bank of the Ounila, the new village across the river (ABH-03, ABH-06, ABH-08).
- Defensive earthen wall with angle towers; one baffled (bent) gate; tall corner-towered houses; mosque, public square, caravanserai, two cemeteries, Sidi Ali ou Amar sanctuary; threshing floors outside the ramparts; agadir/granary at the hilltop (ABH-01, ABH-03, ABH-06).
- Dates: oldest standing fabric 17th c. (UNESCO text); 1987 inscription; 2011 footbridge; granary the first construction (ABH-06, undated).
- Materials: rammed earth (tabia) lower, adobe upper, timber, earth plaster (ABH-03, ABH-08).
- Area about 3.03 ha (ABH-07; ABH-02 says 3.3, unit unstated).
- Agadir hilltop at about 1,300 m (ABH-06). The auto trace DEM at the mapped tomb reads 1273-1296 m (SRTM-derived, about 30 m resolution), so the figures are of the same order.

## Not documented (do not draw as fact)
- Any plan, survey or elevation drawing: none found open. UNESCO documents 216759 and 163062 and the management plan (CRATerre/CERKAS 2007-12) may hold a site plan; not read.
- Positions of individual kasbahs, which towers are the "four corner towers", the gate position, the agadir footprint, the caravanserai, the mosque footprint, wall length/height/thickness, tower heights, storey counts of named houses. Sources give "three or four storeys" for kasbahs only qualitatively (ABH-08, not a survey).
- The number of kasbahs: "several" (ABH-04), six (ABH-05), "five kasbahs" restored (ABH-06). They differ.
- Any date between 17th c. and 1987 for a specific building; any year for the agadir.

## Inferred / assumed in the scene
- Positions of kasbahs, towers, gate and agadir are placed on whatever OSM maps (see the curation file); where OSM maps nothing, the element is flagged inferred and sits on the DEM, not on a plan.
- Heights: all assumed with a rule stated per element (a storey taken as 3.5 m; kasbah 3-4 storeys; enclosure wall about 5 m; tower 1 storey above the house roofline).
- The 17th-century era is the present mapped layout carried back; the ksar was heavily restored for film and heritage use, so today's footprint is not proof of 17th-century plan.
