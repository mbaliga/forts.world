# Korlai: mapped ridge and coastal setting

The old scene was a generic hill, with a guessed gate and shelter. Its catalogue coordinate was about 2 km south of the mapped fort. The current research scene uses 18.5377°N, 72.9074°E as an approximate local projection origin; it is not a survey control.

The fort wall trace follows [OSM way 279967536](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/279967536), tagged both as a historic fort and city wall. Its mapped bounds are roughly 710 m north–south; the extent and line are not an architectural survey, and the rendered wall height and thickness are placeholders. The present [coastline](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/19799065) is clipped on the south and artificially closed across the peninsula; that closure is not an actual coastline. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. The Maharashtra Gazetteer describes a ridge fort, cross-walls and lower outwork; the mapped outline alone does not establish them. Gate locations are unresolved and the displayed trace is closed, so travel through a historically accurate entrance is not yet possible.

The [DGLL master ledger](https://www.dgll.nic.in/sites/default/files/2022-12/mm15.pdf) gives 18°32′15.73″N, 72°54′21.85″E for the lighthouse, and describes a 12.7 m rectangular tower. The scene uses that point and height, with an inferred footprint and simplified appearance. DGLL describes construction in 1955. It is a modern feature, not part of the older fort.

Terrain uses land-only [SRTM30](https://www.opentopodata.org/datasets/srtm/) samples on a 140 m grid, obtained 1 November 2026. Only 16 grid sample centres fall inside the clipped mapped peninsula. Such coarse coverage cannot resolve the steep narrow ridge, walls, sea edge, paths or building foundations. Water level and bathymetry are schematic. Revdanda, the opposite bank and the inhabited village are not reconstructed.

The previous 1610 scene was removed because modern outlines do not establish its form. A measured topographic and architectural survey, registered aerial photographs, wall and gate plans and period-specific evidence are required before calling this reconstruction faithful.
