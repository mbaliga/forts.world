# Mandu published-coordinate registration pass

Date: 31 October 2026
Status: coordinate registration improved; **not a measured 3D reconstruction**.

The 2025 Archaeological Survey of India / National Monuments Authority draft heritage by-laws publish individual GPS positions for 37 protected sites in Mandu, including its gates, palace groups, mosques, tanks, baolis and caves. The playable Mandu scene now projects all 37 published points into its local coordinate frame. The generated terrain extent also includes these markers, so the full published point set is present in the explorable scene rather than clipped beyond the terrain edge.

The markers preserve the names and coordinates in the government record. They are point references only: they do not represent surveyed building footprints, protected-area polygons, wall alignments, heights, or the condition of the remains. Existing building masses, tanks, city housing, and the plateau outline remain schematic. The source describes Mandu as a 20 km² plateau and includes monument survey plans in Annexure I; those plans and the full fortification perimeter still need to be digitized and registered to a common terrain and aerial frame.

The scene retains its 1401–1540 campaign states and present-day state. The GPS labels are research references and do not place later monuments into earlier architectural states as reconstructed buildings. The historic urban fabric and the present settlement footprint remain separate unresolved layers.

## Evidence and next geometry work

- [ASI / NMA 2025 draft heritage by-laws for Mandu](https://asi.nic.in/admin/whatsnew/download/840), pp. 9–10: 37 named monument coordinates; pp. 55–62: ASI survey plans for protected monument groups; pp. 13–14: construction chronology; pp. 26–27: existing residential, public, and tourist uses around the protected groups.
- The current model uses a local metre projection with its declared 3:1 plan compression. Published coordinates determine the markers' relative placement; they do not validate the compression against an orthorectified control layer.
- Next, trace each applicable Annexure-I survey plan at its stated scale, retain the official monument boundary separately from its building footprint, and register those drawings against current terrain and aerial imagery. Only then should the corresponding 3D footprints and local approach routes replace the present schematic blocks.

This pass improves the city's spatial references and terrain coverage. Mandu remains **not verified** until its architectural footprints, full plateau, relief, water system, historic settlement, and present-day settlement have been checked against the drawings and imagery.
