# Pauni Fort (Bhandara, Maharashtra) - research pass 1

Site id `pauni-fort-bhandara-q97147875`, batch 157, 20.78622 N 79.633917 E. Status: research pass 1 only; below T1.

## Documented (secondary sources, not drawings)

- [INHCRF, Pauni early historic fortified settlement](https://inhcrf.org/blogs/pauni-an-important-early-historic-fortified-settlement-and-a-major-hinayana-centre-on-wainganga/): ASI excavation 1994 (Amarendra Nath) found a Mauryan-period (about 4th-2nd c. BCE) laterite-gravel rampart 8.25 m high and 48.90 m wide, with a moat cut from its earth, 55 m wide and 8 m deep. An earlier rampart (6th-4th c. BCE), a brick fortification (1st c. BCE-3rd c. CE) and a Vakataka-period "cyclopean wall" are also recorded. Late medieval fortifications with ashlar gateways still stand.
- Stupas excavated 1968-69 (S.B. Deo, J.P. Joshi): Jagannatha Tekdi, diameter 38.20 m enlarged to 41.2 m; Chandakapur Tekdi, 41.60 m. A third mound, Hardolala Tekdi, is not described.
- [Hitavada, 2021](https://thehitavada.com/Encyc/2021/6/20/ASI-starts-acquisition-work-of-two-ancient-mounds-in-Pauni.html): ASI acquiring two mounds. Not read in detail.

## Conflicts

1. "Pauni Fort" in the queue is the late medieval stone fort; the excavated rampart belongs to the ancient fortified settlement. They are different things on the same ground and must be separate layers.
2. The page says excavations "proved" Hinayana Buddhism, but the proof offered is the stupas, not the rampart.
3. No Gond or Bhonsla attribution for the medieval fort appears on this page; search results suggest one but it is unverified.

## Not found

No rampart brick size, no gate position for either fort, no plan of the late fort, no medieval wall height.
