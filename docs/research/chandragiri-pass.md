# Chandragiri Fort, Kasaragod: research pass

Alpha-U batch, 2026-11-14. Site id `chandragiri-fort-q3520635`. Tier reached **T1** (status `needs-plan`). Curation: `tools/trace/curation/chandragiri-fort-q3520635.mjs`; trace: `src/levels/chandragiri-fort-q3520635.trace.js`. No plan, survey drawing or dated image was found, so nothing here is traced from a plan and no constructed layer is drawn. Standing rules: no invented heights, no invented gates, present-day roads and buildings on the "today" view.

## Mapped (OpenStreetMap and terrain)
- OSM way 799197980 (historic=fort, ruins=yes, Wikidata Q3520635) is the outline: 204 x 237 m, 31,111 m2 (7.69 acres). Wikipedia gives "approximately 7.76 acres" for the boundary, so the mapped area agrees within 1%. This is an independent check, not a survey.
- Sixteen buildings (houses, a club named "fortkochu", Kalanad Old GLPS school), four roads, a farmland zone and a fort marker are mapped around it. No river, sea or trench is mapped in the 1.2 km window.
- Terrain: Terrarium z15 (about 30 m SRTM-derived), 38 m at the outline centroid against the written 46 m (smoothed relief).

## Documented in text (Wikipedia, not placed unless stated)
- Wikipedia: built in the 17th century by Shivappa Nayak of the Keladi Nayakas, at the confluence of the Arabian Sea and the Chandragiri (Payaswini) river, which marked the boundary between Tulunadu and Kolathunadu; about 46 m (150 ft) above sea level.
- Wikipedia: walls of laterite, some damaged by cannon fire; eight bastions with gun holes, the main one on the north; entrances east and west across a trench; a tunnel visible at the north-east corner; managed by the Kerala State Department of Archaeology.

## Inferred
- The 17th-century era is the mapped outline carried back; the page gives no year.

## Conflicts and doubts
- "About seven acres" in the lead against "approximately 7.76 acres" for the boundary and "roughly 5 acres" for the building; the page does not reconcile them.
- Tipu Sultan, the British and "Nayaks of Ikkeri" are not in the page read here. Two reference dates read 2026 and look like typos for 2023.
- This is not the Andhra Pradesh Chandragiri (the old scene in pennar-chandragiri.js); the scene is named "Chandragiri Fort (Kasaragod)" and has its own id.

## Missing evidence for T2
- A Kerala State Archaeology Department survey or plan: the eight bastion positions, the east and west entrances, the trench, the tunnel, wall heights, and the river and sea geometry (re-fetch with a wider window).
