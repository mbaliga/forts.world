# St Angelo, Kannur: mapped fort and shoreline

The previous generic triangle and arbitrarily placed present-day structures have been removed. The current fort outline is [OpenStreetMap way 542192379](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/542192379), v4; the adjacent sea edge follows part of [coastline way 116118921](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/116118921), v21. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. The local and catalogue anchor is now 11.8538 N, 75.3718 E; neither mapped geometry nor the anchor is a measured survey.

The ASI *Inventory of Monuments and Sites of National Importance, Thrissur Circle*, p. 4 ([PDF](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/88324.pdf), PDF page 18), includes a plan and photograph. Its small plan shows the fort and a projecting coastal work; it has not been registered against the OSM polygon. [Kerala Tourism](https://www.keralatourism.org/kerala-article/2020/kannur-fort-where-history-sleeps/1019) describes the north-eastern gateway, jail, stables, church, depot and lighthouses. The gateway opening in this scene is an approximate location beside the mapped present path; its concealed approach is not reconstructed. The sea wall and harbour, moat, fort interior, cannons, tombstones, architecture and phases remain missing. Wall dimensions and the flat terrain are schematic. Three 90 m SRTM samples near the fort from OpenTopodata returned 8, 9 and 6 m, which cannot resolve walls or shoreline grades.

The ASI inventory says the fort passed to the British in 1772, whereas Kerala Tourism says 1790; the discrepancy requires primary historical verification. The pre-existing 1510 generic scene was withdrawn: the current fort outline and later modifications do not establish the earlier fabric.

## Harbour-side shoreline

The scene's bounds now extend east to include a second section of [OSM coastline way 116118921](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/116118921), v21, toward the harbour (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). The mapped edge is present-day and the closure of its water polygon is outside the scene. The historic projecting sea wall has not been identified in the available mapped data and is not reconstructed. Harbour structures, seabed, tide, elevation and the wider bay remain unmodeled. A terrain spot check placed the fort and entrance above the water plane and two selected sea and bay points below it; it does not establish shoreline accuracy or real access.

## Gateway position correction

[OSM way 841300024](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/841300024), v1, maps a band around the ramparts with a gap at the northern tip. [Footway 252404529](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/252404529) crosses that gap. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. The scene's opening has been moved from the previously inferred eastern segment to approximately 9 m east and 130 m north of the local origin, at the mapped crossing. Its width, passage and age are not established by these map layers; this does not constitute a surveyed gate reconstruction. The earlier statement locating the scene opening beside the path is superseded by this correction. A navigation-graph check found connected points on both sides of the opening.

## Rampart footprint pass

The current scene now extrudes the band mapped as [OSM way 841300024](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/841300024), v1 (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL), in place of a constant-width wall centered on the outer site outline. This retains variations in mapped plan width and leaves the mapped entrance gap open. Its flat top and uniform 4 m height above the schematic 8 m ground are placeholders; neither the rampart height, inner masonry face, walk, parapet nor individual phases are surveyed. The label `building=yes` on the OSM way is not by itself an architectural survey. The geometry should be compared with the ASI plan and current photographs before it is considered verified. Navigation graph checks found connected points across the open entrance; that does not verify rampart walkability or visual appearance.
