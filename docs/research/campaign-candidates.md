# Campaign candidates for system validation

This is an engineering and design sequence, not a claim that these will be the first three commercial releases.

| Candidate | Systems it pressures | Current research state | Recommended order |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bajirao–Janjira, 1733 | Operational strategy, camp/settlement supply, forts, mainland combat, coastal craft, blockade, intelligence, monsoon, coalition/command friction. | Campaign outline supported; production details largely unknown. | First systems and historical-content slice. |
| Lachit Borphukan / Saraighat, 1671 | River channels, current and banks, small versus large craft, land-river coordination, river denial, morale/leadership, constrained geography. | High-level official summary reviewed; detailed scholarship and primary sources not yet assembled. | Second, after coastal navigation works. |
| Rajendra Chola maritime expedition, commonly dated 1025 | Ports, open-sea movement, long-distance logistics, multiple destinations, naval uncertainty, amphibious operations, trade and diplomacy. | Candidate only. Inscriptions and modern interpretations must be reviewed directly; routes, goals, vessels, and identifications remain debated or insufficiently established here. | Later, after riverine/coastal systems and content pipeline mature. |
| Mythological campaign, undecided | Land/water/air domains, heroes, powers, creatures, sacred locations, tradition variants, and explicit narrative adaptation. | No source tradition or story selected. | Only after a variant-governance and cultural-review method exists. |

## Janjira rationale

Janjira is unusually useful because failure cannot be explained only by battlefield strength. The initial slice can test whether naval capacity, supplies, terrain, weather, coordination, intelligence, and delegation create understandable consequences across play profiles.

## Saraighat rationale

The Kamrup District, Government of Assam account describes the 1671 Battle of Saraighat as predominantly naval, with larger Mughal boats, smaller Ahom boats, the Brahmaputra, and combined action. That is sufficient to justify research priority, not to design accurate units or tactics. Source: [Government of Assam overview](https://kamrup.assam.gov.in/about-us/battle-alaboi-and-saraighat), source ID `SAR-001`.

Required before production:

- River geography and seasonal hydrology.
- Ahom and Mughal vessels, crews, weapons, formations, command, and logistics.
- Land positions and the relationship between river and bank operations.
- Contemporary or near-contemporary sources and modern critical scholarship.
- Assamese terminology, material culture, architecture, communities, and specialist review.

## Rajendra Chola rationale

The Chola candidate is valuable because it cannot be faked by extending a coastal skirmish map. It requires ports, voyage planning, open-water risk, longer supply, destination intelligence, landing, and diplomacy/trade consequences.

UNESCO and ASI sources establish the broader imperial and architectural context but do not answer naval-mechanics questions. A reviewed 2019 secondary paper points to inscriptional evidence for the Kadaram expedition and also exposes unresolved interpretation and place-identification questions; it remains a discovery source, not production authority. Sources: [UNESCO, Great Living Chola Temples](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/250/), source ID `CHO-001`; [R. Saravanan, expedition to Kadaram](https://ijrar.org/papers/IJRAR19K2550.pdf), source ID `CHO-002`.

Required before production:

- Direct study of relevant inscriptions/copper plates and critical editions.
- Scholarly review of motives, chronology, destinations, outcomes, and disputed identifications.
- Evidence for ports, routes, monsoon navigation, vessel construction, crews, supplies, weapons, and landing practice.
- South Indian and Southeast Asian perspectives rather than a one-sided conquest narrative.
