# Jaipur ridge and Rajasthan living-city fort pass

This pass updates the playable scene context for Amer, Jaigarh, Nahargarh, Taragarh (Bundi), and Mandu. It does **not** certify any site as a faithful reconstruction. The five remain marked **improved geometry; not verified**: no complete fort-wall footprint has yet been registered to a measured plan and terrain model.

## Evidence and scene changes

- **Amer / Amber:** the Rajasthan Tourism description documents Suraj Pol for the normal pedestrian entrance, Chand Pol for car access, the four courts, and a restored public section of the subterranean link to Jaigarh. The 2023 JDA/Forest Department plan identifies the Maota landscape, Kesar Kyari, Dalaram Bagh, Upper and Lower Sagar, Panna Meena Kund, and water bodies at old Amer. Panna Meena Kund now has eight stepped levels and four corner-kiosk blockouts from the state heritage-walk description/photo; the model still marks their local position, footprint, and heights as approximate. Its 20-point forest-land application boundary remains separate from the fort wall; the satellite-derived existing-structure outline is still hand-digitized, not surveyed.
- **Jaigarh:** the published 2023 site sheet distinguishes an existing-structure envelope from the broader precinct, while the municipal source describes a nominal 3 km by 1 km extent. The current scene still uses a dimension-scaled ellipse for that larger extent, so its defensive perimeter is not traced. ASI/IGNCA records three large courtyard tanks supplied by pucca hillside canals; a 2025 CEPT/Jaigarh Trust/CyArk study distinguishes one open and two covered reservoirs. The present scene now represents three schematic reservoirs in a south-courtyard group, with one open and two covered; the tank footprints, placement, and canal courses are not georeferenced. The Amer connection is documented, but its complete alignment remains unmapped.
- **Nahargarh:** the 1734 foundation and later Madhavendra Bhawan expansion remain separate time states. The current Rajasthan Tourism page describes twelve queen’s boudoirs; the 2023 JDA/Forest Department report says nine apartments. The model uses twelve repeated schematic placeholders and preserves the discrepancy in its evidence text. The actual wall, room footprints, ridge terrain, and urban edge remain unresolved.
- **Taragarh, Bundi:** the scene now links the living settlement below the fort with Jait Sagar and Sukh Mahal to the north-east, Nawal Sagar in town, and Raniji ki Baori in the urban fabric. Rajasthan Tourism dates Sukh Mahal to the late eighteenth century and Raniji ki Baori to 1699, and reports the stepwell is 46 m deep. That depth now controls the pit base at the declared vertical scale; its waterline, shorelines, footprints, and placements remain approximate and are not digitized from a survey.
- **Mandu:** the 2025 draft National Monuments Authority heritage bye-laws list protected baolis and tanks; the scene now shows their names as present-period research markers because their individual dates and measured positions are not established here. The 1510 and 1540 scenes also include inferred housing/market massing to represent Mandu as a lived-in fortified city rather than an empty monument compound. A current village/market corridor follows the broad north–south settlement pattern seen in satellite imagery. None of this is a cadastral footprint reconstruction; the full plateau wall and regional terrain remain outside the small encounter map.

## Aerial and map limits

Current Google Maps satellite imagery was visually reviewed for the Jaipur ridge, Bundi town and water relationships, and Mandu’s village-road corridor. The visible ©2026 attribution is not an image-acquisition date. These views provide present-day context only; they do not establish historic ownership, construction phases, or hidden fabric. The state planning sources include 2022-era Google Earth imagery for parts of Bundi and 2023 mapped precinct figures around Jaipur, but they are not current orthophotos or architectural surveys.

The scenes continue to use compact circular battle areas within each site. Those are encounter maps, not footprints of the forts or a substitute for the wider regional geography.

## Follow-up review: 31 October 2026

This pass made five site-specific updates while retaining the **not verified** status for every site:

- **Amer:** corrected the Panna Meena ka Kund cue from the south side of the palace to its north-east approach side, consistent with the current north-up aerial and the state plan’s identification of the stepwell. Its exact local coordinate and footprint are still approximate. The Man Singh Mahal snapshot moved from c. 1610 to c. 1615: the documented 1589–1614 construction interval does not support showing the finished blockout in 1610, so the completed form now enters after the interval ends.
- **Jaigarh:** added the gateway to the Jaivan cannon foundry as a west-facing pointed arch with battlemented parapet, following ASI monument form data. The ASI record’s coordinate fields are malformed and cannot locate it; the model therefore places the gate beside the cannon only as a local relationship cue. The fort’s 3 km × 1 km ellipse remains an extent cue, not a wall trace.
- **Nahargarh:** added the current satellite context to the present-period evidence record; it confirms the palace’s hillside setting above Jaipur’s expanding urban edge, but does not resolve room footprints or the 9-versus-12 suite-count conflict.
- **Taragarh, Bundi:** corrected Nawal Sagar from the east side to the west/south-west of Taragarh, matching the current aerial’s relation to the old town. Shoreline, Varuna temple, and exact water geometry remain unregistered.
- **Mandu:** the c. 1420, 1454, and c. 1480 snapshots no longer show finished blockouts for monuments whose cited construction ranges continue later. The full illustrative forms now enter from the end of their documented intervals: for example, the Jami Masjid at its 1454 completion and Jahaz/Hindola Mahal after the 1469–1500 phase. This conservative date gate avoids implying a finished building existed unchanged at the start of a broad date range; it does not reconstruct construction stages. The current aerial review also confirms the narrow north–south village/road corridor, while the playable scene remains a small encounter area rather than the full fortified plateau.

The current Google Maps tiles displayed 2026 Airbus/Maxar/Google attribution. Their acquisition dates were not exposed, so they were used only to check broad present-day spatial relationships, never to infer historical ownership or hidden architecture. These adjustments improve orientation, provenance, and period separation; they do not make any of the five reconstructions complete, measured, or terrain-ready.

Additional source records: [ASI documentation for Jaivan Cannon Gateway](https://ignca.gov.in/asi_reports/RJJPR_832.pdf); current aerials for [Amer](https://www.google.com/maps/@26.985,75.851,1200m/data=!3m1!1e3), [Bundi](https://www.google.com/maps/@25.444,75.637,1800m/data=!3m1!1e3), [Nahargarh](https://www.google.com/maps/@26.937,75.815,600m/data=!3m1!1e3), [Jaigarh](https://www.google.com/maps/@26.985,75.846,1300m/data=!3m1!1e3), and [Mandu](https://www.google.com/maps/@22.336,75.396,1800m/data=!3m1!1e3), all visually reviewed on 31 October 2026; tile capture dates unknown.

## Mandu coordinate and regional-map pass: 31 October 2026

The 2025 NMA draft bye-laws publish GPS positions for seven protected groups: Ek-Khamba Mahal; Tomb and Mosque Attached; Chor Kot and Chor Kot Mosque; the Darya Khan tomb/mosque/tomb/Somoti Kund group; Hathi Mahal; Caravan Sarai; and Malik Mughis Mosque. The Mandu scene now projects these published coordinates into the expanded local landscape and marks their point positions as documented. This replaces diagrammatic positions for these seven group markers, not their building footprints.

The same government document includes an NMA satellite location map, ASI survey plans with coordinate grids/contours, and archival ASI cadastral drawings (including 1958 plans). Its measured Tomb-and-Mosque description gives a 7.35 m square tomb and a 12.15 × 5.49 m mosque. Those drawings are now catalogued for tracing, but have not yet been digitized into the 3D architecture. The draft’s monument GPS and old survey sheets have not been checked against a modern control survey. The generated plateau relief, 45 km defensive circuit, gates, water and city fabric therefore remain unresolved; the scene is still not a faithful Mandu-wide reconstruction.

Primary source: [NMA/ASI draft heritage bye-laws and survey annexures for Mandu (2025)](https://www.nma.gov.in/showfile.php?lang=2&level=1&lid=1909&ls_id=1551).

## Taragarh, Ajmer: dated repair, waterworks and dismantling phases: 31 October 2026

Har Bilas Sarda’s 1911 *Ajmer: Historical and Descriptive* account adds a dated layer absent from the earlier c.1180-to-today scene. It records repairs by Bithaldas Gor during his Ajmer governorship (1644–1656), describes later Mughal-period superstructure and subsequent Rathor/Maratha alterations, dates Nana Sahib ka Jhalra to 1791 under Governor Shivaji Nana, and says the fort was dismantled in 1832. It names Lakshmi Pol, Phuta Darwaza, Bada/Fateh Darwaza, Bhawani Pol, Hathi Pol, Arkot and multiple bastions and reservoirs. The account’s relative descriptions do not supply a registerable plan for those features.

The scene now separates a c.1644–1656 repair snapshot, a c.1795 Maratha-governor snapshot with the two Nana-associated tanks, and a present ruined-fort snapshot. Its current wall is shown as a low broken silhouette, and historical reservoir names appear as dry research markers with unknown present condition. The lower wall height and all local placements are illustrative; the 1911 statement that the fort was dismantled does not constitute a measured survey of surviving wall fragments. The source reports a total of five reservoirs inside and one outside, but only some can be paired with relative descriptions, so the displayed markers remain approximate and must not be treated as verified coordinates. The scene still lacks a traced perimeter, registered terrain, full gate/bastion placement, and an authoritative reconstruction of earlier ownership and fabric phases.

Primary source: [Har Bilas Sarda, *Ajmer: Historical and Descriptive* (1911), Taragarh chapter](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/14910.pdf); see also [ASI Jodhpur Circle, Gateway of Taragarh Hill](https://www.asijodhpurcircle.in/monument/ajmer/gatewayoftaragarh) and [ASI/IGNCA scaled Lakshmi Pol drawing](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/88329.pdf).

## Jaigarh foundry gateway evidence correction: 31 October 2026

The ASI/IGNCA record for the Main Gate of Cannon Foundry (Kharad Parisar; RJ/JPR-841) documents a south-facing pointed-arch gateway, a one-storey octagonal drilling room 31 ft high, and foundry use from the late sixteenth through nineteenth centuries. A separate inventory (RJ/JPR-832) records the Gateway of Jaivan Cannon-I, a west-facing pointed arch with a kangura parapet. These are distinct gateways, not contradictory descriptions of one opening. The scene now shows both: the west-facing gate by the modeled cannon and a separate south-facing main-foundry gate. Their exact locations and relationship are still inferred; neither ASI sheet supplies a usable registered coordinate or gate footprint, and the drilling-room plan size remains unknown.

Primary sources: [ASI/IGNCA record RJ/JPR-841, Main Gate of Cannon Foundry](https://ignca.gov.in/asi_reports/RJJPR_841.pdf); [ASI/IGNCA record RJ/JPR-832, Gateway of Jaivan Cannon-I](https://ignca.gov.in/asi_reports/RJJPR_832.pdf).

## Amer palace reference-point correction: 31 October 2026

The 2013 State Party nomination for the UNESCO-listed Hill Forts of Rajasthan gives a coordinate for Diwan-i-Aam (P2): 26°59′10″N, 75°51′02″E. The scene now converts that point to its local metre frame and relocates the Diwan-i-Aam blockout to it. The nomination coordinate is a monument reference point, not a measured footprint or orientation; the hall size and other court positions remain schematic. The map still treats the 20-point Amber Museum & Palace application polygon and the JDA existing-structure envelope as distinct from the historic defensive wall.

## Taragarh, Ajmer, area-scale correction: 31 October 2026

ASI Jodhpur Circle describes Taragarh Hill as an approximately 80-acre rocky hill-top fort site. The scene now adjusts its nominal elliptical area to approximately 80 acres at its declared 3:1 plan compression. This constrains total area only; the ellipse does not reproduce the irregular hill edge or the fort wall. The separate Lakshmi Pol gate form continues to follow the ASI scaled drawing, while its placement and the other eight gates remain unresolved.

Primary sources: [ASI Jodhpur Circle, Gateway of Taragarh Hill](https://www.asijodhpurcircle.in/monument/ajmer/gatewayoftaragarh); [ASI/IGNCA scaled Lakshmi Pol drawing](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/88329.pdf).

## Taragarh, Bundi: 1699 stepwell phase: 31 October 2026

The existing playable scene jumped from c.1400 to today, which omitted Raniji ki Baori from the period in which it was built. Rajasthan Tourism dates the stepwell to 1699, attributes it to the younger queen of Rao Raja Anirudh Singh, describes its multi-storey form and carved entrance gateway, and reports a depth of 46 m. The scene now adds a c.1700 state with a tiered tank and three-storey portal massing; the pit's rim-to-floor depth is set to 46 m at the scene's declared 4:1 vertical compression. The waterline remains illustrative because the source gives no dated level. The scene withholds the undated Garh Palace building group from that state. A separate c.1800 state adds Sukh Mahal beside Jait Sagar, following the government tourism chronology that dates it to the late eighteenth century. The site remains unverified: baori shape, footprint, orientation, location and approach are approximate; settlement massing is illustrative, not a historical survey. The c.1400, c.1700, c.1800 and present scenes use different settlement counts, but no historic population or building-density dataset supports those counts.

Primary source: [Rajasthan Tourism, Bundi](https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/bundi.html) and [Raniji ki Baori](https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/raniji-ki-baori.html).

## Mandu: second NMA-measured monument group: 31 October 2026

The 2025 NMA/ASI draft gives one GPS coordinate for the Tomb and Mosque Attached group and lists a 7.35 m square tomb and a 12.15 × 5.49 m mosque. The present-day scene now has separate blockouts for both structures, with those footprints converted to Mandu’s declared 3:1 plan compression. The NMA coordinate is a group position, not a surveyed point for either building; the relative placement, orientation, and heights are inferred. The blocks appear only in the present scene because the cited source does not date their construction. The Hoshang Shah tomb’s documented scale was also reconciled with the same plan compression and the declared 4:1 height compression; the exterior wall allowance remains inferred around the published 14.9 m interior. This is a component-scale correction, not a reconstruction of Mandu’s full 75–82 km² plateau, 45 km fortification, or living settlement.

Primary source: [NMA/ASI draft heritage bye-laws and survey annexures for Mandu (2025)](https://www.nma.gov.in/showfile.php?lang=2&level=1&lid=1909&ls_id=1551).

## Jaipur–Amer and Mandu connected-city evidence: 31 October 2026

This review treats the Jaipur ridge forts, Old Amer, and the walled city of Jaipur as linked but distinct places. UNESCO describes the Jaipur walled city as founded in 1727 and substantially built by 1731 as a planned commercial city in the plain south of the Amber hills. Its defining elements include the grid, nine gates, city wall, bazaars, chaupars, temples, havelis, and active trade. Those features belong in a connected regional layer and dated settlement model; they must not be represented as additional fort precincts or pulled into the compact Amer encounter map. Jaipur's 2025 World Heritage decision says the Special Area Heritage Plan and detailed base-plan inventory remain in progress and notes reports of historic-house demolition. The latest official survey state is therefore not a completed, public parcel-level GIS base that can simply be used as a finished model.

The UNESCO Hill Forts nomination provides an Amber 1:25,000 topographic sheet dated 1975, a satellite-image plate, and separate property/buffer maps. These are useful registration references, but none substitutes for measured architectural footprints; the nominated boundary remains distinct from the inhabited Old Amer settlement and the ridge defenses. The Diwan-i-Aam coordinate is still only a monument reference point.

For Mandu, the ASI-submitted UNESCO tentative-list record states that 61 protected monuments include the fort wall, and gives plan-form, construction, and dimension descriptions for selected monuments. This supports reconstructing a lived fortified plateau with several dated monument clusters, rather than an empty scenic fort. The record does not supply a georeferenced perimeter or enough parcel geometry to validate the current settlement blocks. The NMA/ASI 2025 survey annexures remain the stronger source for monument coordinates and measured components.

These are evidence updates, not fidelity certification. Amer, Jaigarh, Nahargarh, and Mandu remain unverified; current aerials show present-day context only, and the historical city/fort layers still need separate time-filtered footprints, routes, water systems, and buildings.
