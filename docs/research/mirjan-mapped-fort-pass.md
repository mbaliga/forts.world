# Mirjan: mapped wall and moat pass

The generic ten-acre ellipse, four evenly spaced gates, four arbitrarily placed wells and the invented audience hall have been removed. The scene uses [OpenStreetMap wall way 444352229](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/444352229), v3, and [water polygon 444352223](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/444352223), with a present-day [approach path](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/389001408). © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. The local and catalogue anchor moves to 14.4893 N, 74.4177 E, about 250 m west of the former approximate marker. Mapped area and edges are not surveyed dimensions; a second OSM ruins polygon differs in extent, so the older tourism estimate of ten acres is not treated as a verified footprint.

The [ASI excavation report, Indian Archaeology 1999–2000](https://nmma.nic.in/nmma/NAS1/nmma_doc/IAR/Indian%20Archaeology%201999-2000%20A%20Review.pdf), describes a roughly octagonal laterite fort, a moat cut into the ridge, backwaters entering at high tide, and excavation of buried structures. It notes that inscriptions did not establish its builder or date. Its description of a northern main entrance and four side entrances is not fully reconciled with the present eastern mapped path; the scene provides only an approximate eastern crossing, whose form is inferred. All other gate locations remain unmodeled. The assumed 1580 scene was withdrawn.

The water polygon and wall trace are present-day mapping, not proof of historical geometry. Water and floor heights use a convenient local datum and are not surveyed, and the bridge, wall profiles and heights are illustrative. A mapped well is mentioned as an information point; it is not reconstructed. The fort’s river connection, tidal changes, surrounding agricultural land and trade routes, wells, excavation features, masonry details and phase-specific layout require further work. Three 90 m OpenTopodata SRTM samples returned 17, 14 and 16 m; coarse samples cannot determine moat level or tidal elevations.

## Mapped well and paths

A [circular well mapped by OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1457491064), v1, is now shown at its mapped footprint with a schematic rim, water level and depth. The construction and condition are not surveyed. Several present-day mapped interior paths and steps, including [way 444352230](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/444352230), are painted as paths; their width, surface and elevations are illustrative and the mapped steps are not yet reconstructed as flights. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. The ASI report mentions a stepped well on the western side, but no identification between that well and the mapped circular feature has been established. This supersedes the earlier statement that no well is represented.

## Surrounding waterways

OpenStreetMap maps [a waterway west of the fort](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1311195780) and [a stream to its east](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/394876633). Their centre lines are now shown as narrow blue cues in the local scene, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. The width of each cue is a display convention, not a measured channel. Their names, bank geometry, depth, navigability and any connection to the moat have not been established; the lines are not drawn as water polygons or simulated waterways. The ASI report identifies Kudirehalla and the Aghanashini in the larger setting, but these particular mapped lines have not been independently identified as those named waterways. The fort's water level, detailed terrain and seasonal tidal behaviour remain unverified.
