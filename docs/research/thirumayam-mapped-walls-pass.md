# Thirumayam: mapped walls and temple precinct

The earlier scene doubled an arbitrary terrain envelope to represent a district description of the larger historic fort. That did not establish the historic fort's boundary; the invented cave entrances, halls and wall circuit have been removed. The present scene uses [OSM wall ways 583140591](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/583140591), [583140590](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/583140590) and [1002131205](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1002131205), and marks [Sathyamoorthy temple precinct 757306737](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/757306737). © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. These are mapped present-day features, not surveys or a full fort plan. Their heights and two gaps cut into the mapped walls are inferred placeholders for access, not verified entrances. A separately [mapped northern entrance](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/1669711996) is marked as such; its relationship to the district's reported distant historic gateway is unknown.

The [Government Museum Chennai guide](https://govtmuseumchennai.org/uploads/topics/16527952393570.pdf), p. 57, describes rock-cut Shiva and Vishnu temples on the southern slope, western steps, summit gun platform, cisterns and the remains of outer defences. It reports the historical tradition of seven concentric walls, not a measured current plan. The current scene does not reconstruct either shrine or cave, the hall, platform, steps, cisterns, other walls or the town. The hill is a schematic smooth mound: SRTM90m samples at five sites were 106, 98, 101, 96 and 100 m, far too coarse to resolve the rock face or wall elevations. The 1700 scene was withdrawn because present mapped geometry does not establish that phase.

## Elevation grid pass

The invented smooth mound has been replaced with 99 samples from the public [OpenTopodata SRTM 30 m dataset](https://www.opentopodata.org/datasets/srtm/), requested 1 November 2026 at 40 m intervals on an 11-by-9 grid. Local sample centres span x = -200 to 200 m and y = -160 to 160 m relative to 10.2477 N, 78.7512 E; values range from 88 to 113 m and are bilinearly interpolated. The grid is encoded in the level source. Outside the sampled area the scene blends to a 96 m base, so its edges are especially approximate. A 30 m elevation product cannot represent the real rock-cut caves, sheer faces, steps or walls, and its vertical accuracy has not been checked against a ground survey. This supersedes the earlier schematic-mound statement, not the limitations on reconstructing the actual fort and temples.
