# Arnala Fort: research pass

Alpha-U batch, 2026-11-14. Site id `arnala-fort-q3778583`. Tier reached **T1** (status `needs-plan`). Curation: `tools/trace/curation/arnala-fort-q3778583.mjs`; trace: `src/levels/arnala-fort-q3778583.trace.js`. No plan, survey drawing or dated image was found, so nothing here is traced from a plan and no constructed layer is drawn. Standing rules: no invented heights, no invented gates, present-day roads and buildings on the "today" view.

## Mapped (OpenStreetMap and terrain)
- OSM relation 6538186 (historic=castle, Wikidata Q3778583) gives the fort outline: about 239 x 219 m, 38,364 m2 (3.8 ha), with projecting corners. OSM way 122375460 (barrier=city_wall) is the perimeter wall line.
- The island coast is OSM way 22825793 (place=island, fixme=resurvey); the sea is derived from it and passed the DEM check (median 1.8-3.3 m inside, 4.2-5 m outside).
- One building (way 439559695), six living-street paths and a reservoir (way 439559713, 15 x 15 m, 8 vertices) are mapped. Wikipedia describes a "large octagonal freshwater reservoir" (uncited); the 8-vertex shape fits but is not verified.
- Terrain: Terrarium z15 (about 30 m SRTM-derived), 4.8 m at the outline centroid.

## Documented in text (Wikipedia, not placed unless stated)
- Wikipedia: the Marathas took the island after an assault on 28 March 1737 and Peshwa Bajirao I ordered Shankaraji to build a new fort; the Portuguese held the island from the 1530s and a Portuguese nobleman began an uncompleted fort planned at 700 x 700 ft (210 x 210 m); the British captured it on 18 January 1781 and it returned to the Marathas by the Treaty of Salbai.
- Wikipedia: declared a National Protected Monument on 26 May 1909 (uncited); in disrepair.

## Inferred
- The 1737 era is the present mapped outline, wall line, reservoir and coast carried back 290 years. No plan of that date was found.

## Conflicts and doubts
- Maratha rule ends 1817 (text) or 1818 (infobox); the infobox lists a Gujarat Sultanate builder (1516-1530) that the text never supports.
- The Portuguese structure is a "bastion" in one place and a "Martello tower" in another, and is at the west end of the island in one sentence and the south end in another. The 10 Maratha bastions with one standing are uncited and not placed.
- The "three-meter-wide path" on the outer wall is a walkway width, not a wall thickness, and is not used.

## Missing evidence for T2
- A plan or survey drawing of the Maratha fort (the ASI or Maharashtra archaeology department), wall and bastion heights, the positions of the 10 bastions, the north gate (stone doorway with sharabhas and elephants), the plaque, the shrines and dargas, and the Portuguese tower (about 550 m from the fort, uncited).
- Kantak (1993), cited by Wikipedia for the ramparts, is a study of a war and is unlikely to hold a plan.
