# Bekal: mapped fort and coastal setting

The generic keyhole outline, guessed observation tower and tank, and invented tunnel course have been removed. The present scene uses [OSM fort way 670422415](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/670422415), v6, [observation tower way 670422414](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/670422414), v3, and [historic well way 1429695137](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1429695137), plus parts of mapped coastline way 654721435 and footway 670423487. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. The mapped footprint, tower and well are not measured surveys. The tower is a simple mass; its height, ramp and interior are unknown. The well's steps, depth and water level are inferred.

The ASI [Bekal excavation report](https://asi.nic.in/pdf/bekal-excavation_compressed.pdf) describes two lines of walls, 15 bastions, a northern entrance, an observation tower with ramp, water tank, magazine and south-opening tunnels. The ASI Thrissur Circle [inventory](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/88324.pdf), p. 6 (PDF page 20), includes a fort plan and photo; the plan is not yet registered to the mapped outline. Only the mapped perimeter is modeled, with a provisional opening at the northern path. The fort's second wall, actual gate sequence, moat, tower ramp, sea-facing outworks, archaeological features, tunnels and their phases remain to be reconstructed. The 1680 scene was withdrawn because the present layout does not establish its historical state.

The plateau is flat and schematic at 28 m; 90 m OpenTopodata SRTM samples at three points were 28, 26 and 19 m. This cannot resolve the headland cliffs, tank or wall levels. The coastline is a current map trace; sea state and wider settlements are absent. Sources differ over the fort's origins and precise extent; neither the OSM outline nor a general reported acreage is an architectural survey.

## Observation tower access

The ASI excavation report describes a tower ramp, and [OSM way 1429692500](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1429692500) maps an inclined path to the tower (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). The scene now uses that plan direction for a simplified ramp leading to a walkable top on the mapped tower footprint. The ramp width, gradient, material, tower height and top are illustrative, not measured. A navigation test checks connectivity from the courtyard to the tower top; it does not verify the actual route or appearance. This supersedes the earlier statement that no tower ramp is represented.
