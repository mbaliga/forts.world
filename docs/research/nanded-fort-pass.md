# Nanded Fort (Maharashtra) - research pass 1

Site id `nanded-fort-q96394830`, ledger batch 132, alpha-N batch #1, about 19.1417 N 77.3247 E (Wikipedia gives 19°08′30″N 77°19′29″E). Status: research pass only. No geometry, plan or photo has been fetched, so nothing is traced and the tier is below T1.

## Documented (secondary sources, not drawings)

Sources: [Wikipedia, Nanded Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanded_Fort); [Durg Bharari, Nanded](https://durgbharari.in/nanded/).

- Site: on the north bank of the Godavari, in the old part of Nanded city, near the Arab gully by the river (Durg Bharari). Wikipedia says about 4 km from the city; see conflict 1.
- Plan: "double fortification" / double ramparts with 24 bastions originally. Durg Bharari, quoting the gazette, splits them 14 on the outer rampart and 10 on the inner.
- Survival: Wikipedia says 8 bastions in good condition, Durg Bharari says 9 survive (5 visible from outside, 4 from inside). Wikipedia gives bastion height as 18-20 m.
- Material: basalt stone (Wikipedia). Some ramparts newly rebuilt in brick (Durg Bharari).
- Entrance today: one iron gate flanked by two small cement bastions put up by the Nanded Municipal Corporation. This is modern; it is not an original gate.
- Interior features named by Durg Bharari: a Nizam-era cistern of 15 x 12 x 10 ft (now a fountain after a poor renovation), a rock-cut cistern and a stone storehouse below the first bastions, Nizam-era structures on the riverbank partly built from fort material, a cannon on the lower bastion, two municipal water tanks and a rest house beside the remaining bastions. Wikipedia says the fort is now a garden.
- Rulers named (Wikipedia, no construction date given): Bahmani 1358, Ahmad Shah I Wali 1428, Ahmadnagar sultanate, Mughals from 1617, then the Nizam of Hyderabad until 1948.
- Ownership today: Archaeological Department, with an information panel near the water tanks.

## Conflicts

1. Distance: "about 4 km from Nanded city" (Wikipedia) against a site inside the old city (Durg Bharari). Use the coordinates, not the distance.
2. Bastions that survive: 8 (Wikipedia) against 9 (Durg Bharari). Neither is a survey count.
3. Builder: Wikipedia attributes the fort to Muhammad bin Tughluq (Nanded captured 1318) but gives no construction date or source for the fort itself. Durg Bharari says the builder of the walls is not stated. Treat the builder as unknown.
4. Bastion height 18-20 m (Wikipedia) is the only height. It is a tourist-site figure with no source and is not accepted as a wall elevation.
5. Wikipedia's infobox lists 1188 ft as height; Durg Bharari's field reads 0. Neither is usable.

## Not found

- No site plan, ASI or state drawing, or orthophoto located. No wall length, thickness, enclosed area, gate positions or bastion positions from any source.
- The positions of the surviving bastions, the cisterns, the storehouse and the riverbank structures are not documented. Per standing rules no gate is placed and no elevation is invented until a plan gives them.
- Which bastions belong to the outer ring and which to the inner is not documented, only the 14/10 split.
- The river's historic channel position against the fort is not documented.
