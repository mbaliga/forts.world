# Rajasthan city-fort fidelity pass 2

This is a research and scene-update record, not a certification that any model is a faithful reconstruction. The five playable scenes remain **improved geometry; not verified**. Approximate catalogue coordinates are not used as if they were surveyed footprints.

## What changed

- **Jaisalmer:** retained the existing lived-in fort scene and its dated periods. Added all seven well names from the UNESCO-hosted Site Management Plan as explicitly approximate markers. Replaced the earlier generic four-direction rain-drain statement with the plan’s account of three original ghutnallis and a fourth outlet added with the entrance road. Drain routes are not traced.
- **Mehrangarh:** retained the detailed ramp, gate and palace blockout and added Ranisar and Padmasar as nearby water landscape cues. Rajasthan Tourism places the lakes near Fateh Pol; the scene shorelines and coordinates remain approximate.
- **Junagarh, Bikaner:** removed the misleading hill-fort terrain and replaced it with flat ground and a compact rectangular enclosure sized as a first-pass approximation. Added Suraj Pol and Karan Pol cues, named palace groups from government sources, and surrounding illustrative Bikaner blocks. Neither the perimeter nor the palace/city footprints are georeferenced.
- **Taragarh, Bundi:** added schematic Chhatra Mahal, Phool Mahal, Badal Mahal and Chitrashala blocks, plus settlement massing on the eastern side. Linked the regional stepwell context to Raniji ki Baori without asserting its position relative to the fort.
- **Lohagarh, Bharatpur:** replaced the oversized square with a clipped-corner rectangle closer to the visible urban fort form, retained the ASI inventory’s nominal 18.3 m moat width at the scene scale, and added named gate, palace and burj cues with surrounding settlement blocks. Named placements, gate bearings and current waterline remain unverified.

The Jaisalmer 1650 phase now uses the Government-submitted UNESCO Site Management Plan for its documented foundation/completion sequence, 13th–14th-century defensive construction, 15th-century Jain/merchant settlement phase and later Mughal influence, instead of relying on a tertiary summary for the main historical basis. The model still does not assign a verified date to each bastion or place the 1650 house lots from a georeferenced plan.

## Evidence used and limits

Government of Rajasthan tourism pages describe Jaisalmer as a living fort with residents and commercial uses; the UNESCO-hosted Jaisalmer Site Management Plan names seven wells and describes the historic drainage system. Rajasthan Tourism describes Ranisar and Padmasar near Mehrangarh’s Fateh Pol. Rajasthan Tourism attributes Junagarh to Raja Rai Singh in 1588 and describes its palace complex; the Government of India’s Incredible India page gives a rectangular plan, while the Bikaner district census handbook lists Suraj Pol and palace names. Rajasthan Tourism describes Bundi’s Taragarh and multi-century Garh Palace, and dates Raniji ki Baori to 1699. An ASI inventory accessed through IGNCA describes Lohagarh’s rectangular plan, eight bastions, two gates and moat; Government of Rajasthan material supplies additional gate and monument names.

Current aerial imagery was visually inspected for broad setting and outline relationships. This was not an orthorectified survey, coordinate registration, cadastral comparison, or measurement of building footprints. Aerial appearance alone was not treated as evidence for historic ownership, construction dates, or hidden/changed fabric. The model positions and urban blocks added in this pass are visibly labeled as approximate or schematic where possible.

The principal remaining work is to obtain or extract legible site plans and conservation drawings, align them to current orthophotography/terrain, record map scale and control points, and split each site into dated ownership and construction phases. Until those steps are complete, do not promote these scenes to “verified” or use them as terrain-ready campaign coordinates.
