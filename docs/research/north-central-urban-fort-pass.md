# North-central urban-fort pass: Gwalior, Delhi and Agra

This is a source and scene audit for five linked sites, not a claim that their 3D geometry is now faithful. The scene prototypes remain playable, but the audit classifies all five as **IMPROVED GEOMETRY; NOT VERIFIED**. None has yet had a measured plan or current aerial orthorectified into its local scene coordinate system.

## Gwalior Fort

The Madhya Pradesh / Government of India integrated master plan describes Gopachal as a sandstone outcrop with fort walls, gates, stepwells, tanks, temples, a gurdwara and a mix of ASI- and state-protected monuments. It records reserve forest on three sides and an urban setting. The National Monuments Authority satellite map labels the northern and southern monument clusters and names the gates, palace, temples and Jain colossi. The Gwalior Division government page describes the steep sandstone hill, long outer walls, Jain rock-cut approach and Gujari Mahal. Together these are much stronger controls than the scene's old local hand-drawn alignment, but the satellite image and structure points have not been registered to the model.

The existing hand-authored Gwalior scene is substantially more detailed than the four generated enclosures in this pass. It has a plateau and cliff model, two approaches, named monuments and distinct 1780 / 1868 / present eras. Its important unresolved risk is that the local paths, walls, buildings and point placements are still model coordinates informed by source reading, not a georeferenced trace. The 1780 escalade face is explicitly a guess. The fort's surrounding living city and reserve forest are also not represented as real terrain or current settlement fabric. Several POI descriptions still rely on secondary or crowd-sourced references and require source-by-source replacement with ASI/state records before treating their details as verified.

## Red Fort and Salimgarh, Delhi

The UNESCO Red Fort map places the fort within Shahjahanabad and labels the historic city wall, Yamuna, Jama Masjid, gates and Salimgarh. UNESCO describes the Red Fort as Shah Jahan's palace fort and Salimgarh as the older Suri fort built in 1546; it discusses the two as one complex in later use and history. The same evaluation documents major changes: the former river setting became a road, and a railway cuts through Salimgarh.

The current scenes still render Red Fort and Salimgarh as separate generated enclosures. Their era text and POIs now make the connections and uncertainties explicit, and the Red Fort prototype records its Shahjahanabad relationship and British/post-1857 layers. The scenes do not yet include the mapped city, actual palace grid, river edge, road or railway. No aerial was compared in this pass. A future shared Delhi landscape should register the UNESCO nomination map, current orthophoto/aerial, ASI plans and dated city maps before shaping the walls and waterfront.

## Agra Fort

UNESCO describes a 2.5 km enclosure, a semicircular plan beside the Yamuna and an imperial city within the fort, including named palaces, audience halls and mosques. Its map record supplies the official property coordinate/map reference. The current scene now exposes those facts and preserves the separation between Akbar's sixteenth-century phase and later Shah Jahan structures, but its generated oval is only a prototype: it is not a 2.5 km measured outline, does not place the palaces, and has no riverbank or Agra city.

## Purana Qila

Delhi Tourism describes a roughly rectangular enclosure, three gateways, bastions and a moat formerly connected to the Yamuna, with works associated with Humayun and Sher Shah Suri. The ASI protected-monument inventory adds the walls, arcades, gateways, bastions, garden, Sher Shah's mosque and Sher Mandal. The scene annotations now expose this feature inventory and period uncertainty. Its square wall and moat remain generic; no feature has been plotted from a measured plan and no present aerial comparison has been completed.

## Evidence and next reconstruction steps

| Site | Evidence reviewed in this pass | Scene state | Main blocker to faithful reconstruction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gwalior | Government integrated master plan; NMA satellite monument map; state division heritage page | Detailed hand-authored but unregistered model | Georeference the satellite/monument map and current aerial; reconcile local coordinates, fort circuit, approaches, monuments and settlement |
| Red Fort | UNESCO property map and Red Fort nomination/evaluation | Generated octagonal prototype | Digitize official property boundary and palace plan, register Shahjahanabad/Yamuna and later road/railway to aerial |
| Salimgarh | UNESCO Red Fort complex page and evaluation map | Generated island-fort prototype | Trace former river island, bridge and later railway, then join it to Red Fort as one landscape |
| Agra Fort | UNESCO property page, map record and fluvial-fort description | Generated oval prototype | Digitize the semicircular enclosure and river edge, trace palace-city blocks and validate 2.5 km perimeter |
| Purana Qila | Delhi Tourism description and ASI protected-monument inventory | Generated square/moat prototype | Obtain/trace ASI plan and archaeological phasing; place three gates, moat, mosque and Sher Mandal against current aerial |

For each site, “current aerial comparison” remains **not completed** unless stated otherwise above. The government/NMA satellite sheet is contextual evidence for Gwalior clusters, not proof that every model vertex or current building condition is accurate. Aerial imagery must be dated, licensed and registered against stable control points before it is used to assert footprints or present-day condition.
