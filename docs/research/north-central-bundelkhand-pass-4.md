# Gwalior–Bundelkhand connected-site pass 4

Reviewed 2026-10-31. This is a source and scene reconciliation pass, not a claim that any site is a survey-grade or photo-real reconstruction. Current aerial imagery was visually inspected in a map viewer; its capture date and positional accuracy were not established, and it was not downloaded or registered to the game coordinate system.

## Gwalior

The existing hand-authored scene is the strongest model in this group. Its 1780, 1868 and present-day states draw on official plans, monument inventories, archival views and photographs. UNESCO's 2024 Tentative List entry gives a fort-region reference coordinate (26.2313, 78.1695), documents the eastern ascent and six gates, names the Hathi Pol, Alamgiri/Gwalior Gate and reservoirs, and records the multi-period monument ensemble. Current aerial inspection confirms the long sandstone plateau among the city. The local scene has not been registered to its source satellite plan; its perimeter, contemporary city footprints, reservoir shapes and all terrain remain unverified. Source: [UNESCO Gwalior Fort tentative-list dossier](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6730).

## Jhansi

The district government identifies the rocky Bangra hill within Jhansi city, names ten gates and records Karak Bijli tank, Rani Jhansi Garden, a Shiva temple and memorials. It attributes the fort to Raja Bir Singh Ju Deo (1606–27); the district history's more specific 1613 date is retained as a distinct claim rather than treated as a surveyed construction date. The scene now communicates the documented city-fort relationship with surrounding urban blocks. They are gameplay context, not traced current streets or buildings. None of the ten gateways or internal monuments has been placed from an authoritative measured plan. The official photo gallery is catalogued for later image-by-image review. Sources: [Jhansi District Fort page](https://jhansi.nic.in/tourist-place/jhansi-fort/), [Jhansi Fort photo gallery](https://jhansi.nic.in/gallery/jhansi-fort/).

## Orchha

The ASI-submitted UNESCO tentative-list dossier describes the fortification, living town, gardens, river and forest as an integrated cultural landscape, and identifies the Raja Mahal, Jahangir Mahal and Rani Mahal (now Ram Raja Temple) as a monumental ensemble. Niwari District confirms the Betwa/Satdhara seven-channel association. Current aerial inspection confirms the palace cluster beside the Betwa and its channels; it does not establish the exact historic water geometry or date the imagery. The present scene remains deliberately schematic: palace footprints, settlement blocks, river branches and access routes are not traced or terrain-registered. Sources: [UNESCO Orchha dossier](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6404/), [Niwari District Orchha page](https://niwari.nic.in/en/tourist-place/orchha/).

## Narwar

The most useful new evidence is the Government of Gwalior State's 1923–24 archaeological report, now digitized through the Archaeological Survey of India library. It records the east road through town, a west approach, a now-closed north-west Dholya Pol road, six named gates, the palace names Kachahri, Ladau Bangala and Chhip Mahal, and five tanks. D. R. Patil's 1952 government heritage survey describes four fortified enclosures—Maj-loka, Madar Ahata, Gujar Ahata and Dhola Ahata—and the fort's relationship to the Sindh valley. Those names now replace generic labels in the present-day scene; c.1250 uses one generic opening and does not inherit the named palace/enclosure forms. The historical records do not supply a registered plan for these scene locations; the model's gate bearings, enclosure outlines, terrain, town and road geometry are not measured. The old reports include period-specific condition descriptions that must not be mistaken for current condition. Sources: [Gwalior State Archaeological Department annual report, 1923–24](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/22657.pdf), [D. R. Patil, *The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat* (1952)](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/1284.pdf), [Narwar section of the 1923–24 report](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/27344.pdf).

## Kalinjar

Banda District lists the temple, palace, tanks and other named sites and records the reported sequence from Chandela control through Akbar's 1569 capture, Bundela control and British occupation. The Government of India's CCRT fort volume describes seven gates by name and reports overall fort dimensions of about 2 km east–west by 1 km north–south. The present-day scene carries all seven gate names and cites the approximate dimensions without claiming its generated perimeter matches them; the 1185 and 1580 scenes use one generic opening because the names have not been dated to those snapshots. Gate bearings, fort wall, Neelkanth temple, water sites, palaces, nearby basti and forested terrain still need georeferenced survey/map evidence. Current aerial inspection is broad visual context only. Sources: [Banda District Kalinjar Fort page](https://banda.nic.in/tourist-place/kalinjar-fort/), [CCRT, *Forts and Palaces of Madhya Pradesh*](https://ccrtindia.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Pub_Forts_MP.pdf).

## Model status

All five are playable generated scenes, but no site in this group should be described as complete or fully verified. Gwalior has the richest documented reconstruction and period-specific visual references; the other four remain inferred prototypes with better source coverage and more explicit feature names. In every case, a named gate, monument, terrain cue or settlement block is not proof of accurate placement. The next work that could materially raise fidelity is georeferencing official site plans and monument inventories against a dated orthophoto/DEM, then splitting each site into evidence-backed architectural and ownership phases.
