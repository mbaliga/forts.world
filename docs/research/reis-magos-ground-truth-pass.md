# Reis Magos: correction after source and plan review

The earlier model was not a faithful reconstruction. It used a generic oval hill fort, placed 33 guns around an invented ring, and invented the forms and local positions of the church and spring. The 33-gun statement in the Goa Government account does not provide gun locations, an exact inventory date or evidence for the previous 1710 scene. The old scene should not have been presented as an accurate period model.

The replacement is a present-day research reconstruction. Its perimeter uses [OpenStreetMap way 194550880, version 12](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/194550880), mapped in local metres from 15.496500°N, 73.809130°E. The OSM way was last edited on 9 July 2024 and is tagged as derived from Bing satellite imagery; that timestamp is not the imagery date or survey accuracy. © OpenStreetMap contributors, [ODbL](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). Its extent is about 123 m east–west by 136 m north–south; the former generator’s nominal footprint was approximately 468 m by 360 m before irregularity.

The silhouette and two-level arrangement were visually checked against figure 7 of [Nuno Lopes, *Goa, uma perspectiva territorial de defesa (1510–1660)*](https://institutodehistoriadaarte.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/rha_13.pdf), p. 98, which overlays features on Google Earth imagery dated 2014 and supplies a 0–60 m scale. The plan identifies upper bastions, lower bastions at the Mandovi, and the two connecting walls. [Goa Tourism](https://goa-tourism.com/fort/reis-magos-fort/) describes laterite walls, turrets and modern restoration. The scene uses the mapped perimeter and a north-to-south slope, with a suggested entrance and two approximate lower pavilion masses.

The source polygon is a mapped outline, not a measured wall survey; it does not establish wall thickness, precise bastion construction, gateways, rooms, roof forms or individual monument phases. Elevation, riverbank and pavilions in the scene are approximate; the present ground and restored structures must not be projected unchanged into 1710. The 1710 scene was removed until dated site plans and architectural evidence support it. The church and spring are not modeled: Goa Government sources differ on whether the spring is east of or within the fort, and neither source establishes a mapped position in this pass. Find the restoration survey, dated elevations, photographs and a current orthophoto/terrain control before claiming accuracy or completion.

## Present-day surroundings pass

The local scene now includes the mapped outline of [Reis Magos Church](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/618726579) (way version 5), the [cemetery](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/618726580) (v1), [church steps](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/618726581) (v1), and a local section of the [Mandovi shoreline](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/590981194) (v4). © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. The government describes the [church and its setting](https://www.goa.gov.in/places/the-reis-magos-church/); its [fort account](https://www.goa.gov.in/places/reis-magos-fort/) notes a flight of stairs to the church. These sources establish neither a survey nor all architectural forms. The church is a generic mass on a mapped footprint; its façade, interior, roof details, stair rise and cemetery features are not reconstructed. The church steps are not the fort's internal stairways.

Five 90 m SRTM samples obtained through OpenTopodata return 22 m at the fort anchor, 20 m at the church, 16 m near the mapped reception, 8 m near the lower fort and 17 m further north. Such coarse samples do not resolve building elevations, slopes or stair flights; the existing slope and new church stair rise remain illustrative, not a DEM reconstruction. The mapped shoreline and features are present-day data, not proof of their historic geometry or extent.

The Mandovi connects this place with Aguada and Old Goa on the regional campaign map. The locations and routes there remain approximate, and the game does not yet provide a geographically continuous explorable Goa, surveyed river crossings, river shipping, the nearby built settlement, the actual entry and ramp sequence, fort stairways, gun positions, or reconstructed church and cemetery. The earlier statement that the church is not modeled is superseded by this provisional mass; no other earlier caveats are superseded.

## Approach and level connection

The present scene paints a portion of [Reis Magos Road](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/330035641) and the [eastern pedestrian approach](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1155294094), using their mapped centre lines. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Width, surfacing and terrain here are illustrative, and the lines do not establish historical routes. [Kiperman's field study](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/files/41019549/THESIS_MASTER_BY_RESEARCH_KIPERMAN_Flavia_2019.pdf), pp. 183–184, describes the steep eastern pedestrian road and a steep staircase between the fort's upper and waterside parts. A stair has been added between the levels as an explicit inferred placeholder: the study does not supply its plan, width, number of flights or elevations. Neither the painted approach nor this staircase should be mistaken for a measured restoration. The rest of the fort's internal stairs, ramps, architectural details and real terrain still need a survey and detailed photographic control.

The scene also traces the present [mapped retaining wall beside the river road](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1155270354) (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). This is not identified as a historical fortification: its date, height, thickness and construction remain unknown, and the displayed dimensions are illustrative. A navigation-graph check found connected nodes near the upper fort and lower battery; it does not prove the actual stair geometry or accessibility.

## Coarse elevation baseline

The uniform invented slope has been replaced with 132 samples from the public [SRTM30 dataset through OpenTopodata](https://www.opentopodata.org/datasets/srtm/), collected on 1 November 2026 at 40 m spacing across approximately 400 × 440 m around 15.4965°N, 73.80913°E. Heights are interpolated between samples and span 0–41 m; the mapped river polygon is separately cut to a schematic water level. This data is far too coarse to resolve the real fort's terraces, ramps, stair flights, retaining walls, church platform, waterline or building foundations. Some samples can include surface features, and ocean/river values are not bathymetric measurements. The surrounding terrain at the scene boundary is still an artificial edge. No historical topography or change in sea level is implied. The church mass, its approach and the provisional internal stair now use the approximate sampled ground rather than the earlier arbitrary linear-slope heights; this does not make their architectural geometry accurate.

The [present visitor entry/reception footprint](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/618724201) is shown as a labelled outline beside the eastern approach, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. It is not a reconstructed building or evidence of the historic entrance.


## Additional church and lower-fort approaches

The present scene now includes mapped modern approach segments: [the road by the church](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/618724203), [church footpath](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/618726582), and [lower-fort path](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/618724204), plus a labelled outline for the [church parish centre area](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1155270366). © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Their displayed widths and surfaces are schematic; the parish centre is a land-use outline rather than a building footprint. These features do not establish routes or buildings in an earlier campaign. They improve local context but do not yet make the terrain, interior or wider Goa landscape complete.

The Goa government identifies [Aguada](https://www.goa.gov.in/places/reis-magos-fort/) near Reis Magos and lists [Chapora, Corjuem, Colvale and Cabo](https://www.goa.gov.in/what_to_see/forts/) among Goa's forts. Goa Tourism describes the [former Gaspar Dias fort](https://goa-tourism.com/guide/miramar-beach/) at Miramar. These sites belong in a regional evidence and survey backlog; their presence in a catalogue does not establish their period-specific geometry or continuous 3D terrain between them.


## Single-fort completion review: restored Reis Magos

A [photograph of the on-site citadel plan](https://independent-travellers.com/india/goa/reis_magos_fort/12.php) identifies Hall 2, a craft centre and the surrounding ramparts; neither hall is reconstructed in the scene. The same photographic record includes [a restoration exhibit and physical model](https://independent-travellers.com/india/goa/reis_magos_fort/54.php), [the descent](https://independent-travellers.com/india/goa/reis_magos_fort/58.php), [lower-level stairs](https://independent-travellers.com/india/goa/reis_magos_fort/61.php), and [buildings and a watchtower](https://independent-travellers.com/india/goa/reis_magos_fort/75.php). These are visual references to a restored present-day site, not a dimensioned site survey; their viewing dates and scale are not established here. The 2006 exhibit is explicitly an initial scheme and is not evidence that every modeled proposal was built.

**Not complete and not ready for approval.** Before presenting this fort for a completion review, locate or obtain registered conservation plans and elevations, confirm their permitted use and observation date, fit every wall, upper-citadel building and lower battery to the mapped site, reconstruct and test the actual stair/gate routes and levels, check the church, cemetery, slopes and surrounding river setting against suitable imagery, and independently compare the finished model with photographs. The current coarse elevation and provisional stair and pavilions cannot establish these details. Establish a separate evidenced period before depicting any earlier campaign.

## Church staircase visual correction

The [photograph of the church frontage](https://independent-travellers.com/india/goa/reis_magos_fort/1.php) shows a broad flight of steps in front of the church. The scene now represents a broad flight along the mapped step line; its 20 m width is an estimate, not a measurement from the photograph or OpenStreetMap. Its alignment, height, number of steps, railings and relationship to the façade still need survey control. The coarse terrain cannot establish its rise. This is the church approach, not the fort's internal staircase, and this correction does not make the site complete.
