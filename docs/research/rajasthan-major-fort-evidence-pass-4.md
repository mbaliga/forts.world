# Rajasthan major-fort evidence pass 4

This pass prioritizes connected, inhabited fort settings and measured architectural components over adding more catalogue markers. It corrects five existing scenes using ASI, UNESCO, Rajasthan government and AAI evidence. These remain research-stage reconstructions: no full-site scene is certified accurate or terrain-registered.

## Chittorgarh

ASI’s inventory describes Kirti Stambh as about 24 m high, with a square platform, a central stair through six storeys and an open summit pavilion supported by 12 pillars. The scene now uses a 9 m base, six-storey shaft, and 4.6 m square pavilion represented by 12 columns. This changes one measured component only; its scene position is still schematic. The same inventory reports an inscription dated V.S.952/A.D.896 but also argues that style and attribution point to the 13th–14th century. The conflict is kept unresolved rather than assigning one date as settled. Source: [ASI Inventory of Monuments, Chittorgarh section](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/88329.pdf).

## Kumbhalgarh

The primary ASI inventory entry 130 (printed pp. 171–172) now provides the key source needed for a wall-trace repair: a whole-fort site plan with a metric scale, perimeter outline, and labeled internal features. The same entry dates Rana Kumbha's construction to 1443–1458, notes an older castle tradition, lists major temples, and distinguishes Badal Mahal as later. It describes a series of walls with battlements and bastions and says seven great gateways, but the sentence names only six (Aret, Halla, Hanuman, Ram, Nimboo, Bhairon Pol). The missing name must remain an evidence gap until the full plan or another authoritative record resolves it. The later Fateh Singh palace is also not part of the 15th-century state.

ASI records that Neelkantha Mahadeva, Ganesh, and Charbhuja remain under worship; the scene's religious-use layer should preserve active use in the present period. Other temples require their own construction phase and footprint from the inventory sub-entries rather than being cloned or treated as generic fort buildings.

ASI’s monument inventory gives dimensions, facing and bastion form for the nine named gates. The local gate passages now use the published widths; Hanuman Pol’s bastions are semicircular rather than octagonal. Their placements and approach alignment are not surveyed in the scene, so the gate corrections do not validate the full fort. The UNESCO conservation report describes a 14 km wall while Rajasthan Tourism advertises 36 km; these different claims remain in the source record and are not silently combined. Sources: [ASI Inventory of Monuments](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/88329.pdf), [UNESCO conservation report](https://whc.unesco.org/document/218301), [Rajasthan Tourism](https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/kumbhalgarh-fort.html).

### Wall geometry correction and remaining perimeter discrepancy: 31 October 2026

The former hand-authored ring was 1,713.6 local units (about 5.14 km at 3 m/unit), too broad east–west and too short to represent the mapped circuit. A research candidate was derived from OpenStreetMap way 254041054, tagged `historic=fort` and `barrier=city_wall`. The source way has 1,489 vertices; a 3 m Douglas–Peucker tolerance reduces it to 419 vertices. Projected at the scene's declared 3 m/unit scale from the OSM Ram Pol point, the simplified circuit is about 9.64 km (the unsimplified source measures about 9.88 km), and the source polygon area is about 241 ha. SRTM90 elevation samples were collected for the trace. The candidate is not active in the playable scene: the mapped entrance and approach did not retain nav connectivity in the three period scenes, and the perimeter-length discrepancy remains unresolved. Keep it as a research trace, not as a verified replacement outline.

The mapped perimeter remains in conflict with the UNESCO conservation report's approximately 14 km main-wall figure. UNESCO's 268 ha nominated-property area is a separate boundary measure, not an exact wall area; it must not be silently substituted for either measurement. The ASI inventory entry 130 whole-fort plan supports the broad north–south irregular outline, and UNESCO nomination Maps 1.9, 1.15 and 1.21 provide topographic, satellite and property-boundary context. The OSM trace and Ram Pol coordinate are retained as research references, but neither has been adopted in the playable scene. The Vedi centroid is likewise a current-map reference, not a measured architectural placement. The internal plan, approach gates, summit, other temples, tanks, settlement and routes have not yet been registered together. The OSM trace may follow the mapped UNESCO wall boundary rather than every defensive wall segment described by ICOMOS; reconcile that distinction and restore playable access before integration.

Sources: [OpenStreetMap way 254041054](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/254041054) (ODbL; 2026-10-31 extract), [SRTM90 elevation samples](https://api.opentopodata.org/v1/srtm90m), [ASI inventory entry 130](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/88329.pdf), [UNESCO conservation report](https://whc.unesco.org/document/218301), and [UNESCO nomination maps](https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/247rev.pdf).

### Official map controls reviewed: 31 October 2026

The UNESCO nomination dossier adds Map 1.9 (topography), Map 1.15 (satellite image), and Map 1.21 (nominated property and buffer zone). Its map index identifies the topographic base as a 1:250,000 sheet dated 1977. These sheets establish broad location, ridge context, and the distinction between nominated-property boundary and fort fabric; the published dossier does not provide usable image acquisition metadata or georeferencing control for tracing individual wall vertices. They therefore strengthen the evidence set without making the full local scene terrain-ready. The OSM trace candidate is a current mapped boundary, not an official archaeological survey, and the SRTM90 samples are too coarse to establish individual gate and building positions. A first integration attempt broke route connectivity in all three eras, so the trace remains outside the playable build.

### Vedi Temple Complex placement correction: 31 October 2026

The current OSM Vedi religious-site polygon (way 252292380) and named information node place the complex about 72 m east and 1 m south of the mapped Ram Pol coordinate. The research candidate placed its platform centre about 36 m east and 61 m north of the OSM centroid before an experimental shift. That shift is not present in the playable scene. The mapped centroid and SRTM90 sample elevation (996 m) are references only; individual structure positions still need georeferencing from the ASI subplans and current imagery.

Sources: [OpenStreetMap Vedi complex](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/252292380) (ODbL; 2026-10-31 extract), [SRTM90 elevation samples](https://api.opentopodata.org/v1/srtm90m), and [ASI inventory](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/88329.pdf).

Primary source: [UNESCO nomination dossier, Hill Forts of Rajasthan, Maps 1.9, 1.15 and 1.21](https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/247rev.pdf).

Source image: [photograph of the ASI-installed Kumbhalgarh map](https://anandbharat.com/kumbhalgarh-fort-a-unesco-world-heritage/); primary measured plan: [ASI inventory of protected monuments, entry 130 (whole fort)](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/88329.pdf); dimensional references: [UNESCO Hill Forts of Rajasthan maps and component areas](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247/maps/), [UNESCO Detailed Conservation Report: Kumbhalgarh](https://whc.unesco.org/document/218301).

### Bawan Devri: measured plan correction, 31 October 2026

ASI inventory entry 130(a), printed p.173, provides a scaled plan with a 0–10 m bar and describes a 52-shrine Jain complex: fifty small cells around the perimeter, one larger shrine in the back wall, and one larger shrine in the courtyard. It states there is one entrance from the north and records the complex is not currently in religious use. Reading the scanned plan gives an approximate outside footprint of 28 x 56 m. The scene previously represented a 28 x 20 local-unit east-west compound, 49 cells, and two large shrines beside one another. It now uses a north-south 31 x 62 local-unit enclosure at the declared 0.9 m per unit plan scale, exactly fifty small cells, a north entrance gap, the rear shrine and central shrine in separate positions, and ten ruined cells today (about forty survive per the source). The 1820 scene no longer asserts undocumented cell losses.

This corrects the plan’s axis and broad proportions, not the full temple architecture. The original scan is low resolution; the dimensions and small-cell arrangement remain scale-read approximations, and the complex is not georeferenced to the whole-fort plan or terrain. The Kumbhalgarh fort remains **not verified** until its outer wall network and all other structure positions are registered.

Primary source: [ASI inventory entry 130(a), Bawan Devri plan (printed p.173)](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/88329.pdf#page=192).

### Neelkanth Mahadev: scaled mandapa correction, 31 October 2026

ASI inventory entry 130(b), printed p.174, gives a scaled plan and site photograph. It describes a raised platform reached from the west, an open pillared mandapa around the shrine, a sarvatobhadra sanctum with entrances on all four sides, and Nandi before the western opening. The scanned 0–10 m scale reads to approximately 26 x 38 m outside. The scene replaces its 15 x 13 local-unit east-west block and unsupported seven domes with a 29 x 42 local-unit north-south pillared mandapa, a west-side stair, and one central sanctum block. The 0.9 m/local-unit conversion follows this scene's scale convention.

This is a scale-read component correction. Exact column centers, sanctum doorways, the sculpted Nandi, and georeferencing to the whole-fort map remain incomplete; the site is still unverified.

Primary source: [ASI inventory entry 130(b), Neelkanth Mahadev plan and photograph (printed p.174)](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/88329.pdf#page=193).

### Mandu: Hindola Mahal plan-form correction, 31 October 2026

ASI describes Hindola Mahal as a T-shaped palace: a long hall forms the stem, with tall side arches and sloping buttresses; a later double-storey structure forms the cross-bar. The scene previously reduced it to one rectangle. It now separates the long-hall and cross-bar masses so the defining plan reads as a T. The mass sizes, arch rhythm, buttress profiles and relative placement are not measured or registered from a scaled plan, so this remains a form-level correction only. Both masses enter conservatively after the documented 1469–1500 phase; the exact date of the later cross-bar is unresolved. Mandu remains unverified at city scale.

Primary source: [ASI architectural description of Mandu monuments](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/37526.pdf).

### Amer: Man Singh Mahal courtyard orientation correction, 31 October 2026

The UNESCO/ICOMOS evaluation describes Amber Palace as arranged along an almost north–south axis, identifies Man Singh Mahal as the fourth and southern courtyard, and describes the second and third courts to its north. The schematic Man Singh Mahal block was north of the Diwan-i-Aam reference point in the scene, contradicting that sequence. It has been moved south. This repairs the broad relational error only: the court footprint, other court positions, stairs, connections and orientation still need to be registered from the nomination sheets and current aerial controls.

Primary source: [ICOMOS evaluation of the Hill Forts of Rajasthan nomination](https://whc.unesco.org/document/116681).

### Gagron: north-east two-gate approach correction, 31 October 2026

The UNESCO/ICOMOS evaluation describes Gagron on a rocky plateau at the Ahu–Kali Sindh confluence and identifies the principal approach from the north-east through a steep passage crossing two gates. The scene previously had one south-facing landward gate. It now shows two gates on the north-east side. The exact gate coordinates, spacing, route and fort wall are still inferred; this fixes the documented access direction and gate count only, and does not make the river terrain survey-accurate.

Primary source: [UNESCO/ICOMOS evaluation of the Hill Forts of Rajasthan nomination](https://whc.unesco.org/document/153037).

### Ranthambore: stepped Z-shaped gateway correction, 31 October 2026

ASI Jaipur Circle describes Ranthambhore's fortification as including a stepped, Z-shaped gateway with two strong doors. The earlier scene showed only the generic pointed-arch opening generated at the north wall. It now models that opening as the outer door, adds offset inner-door and cheek-wall geometry, and labels the internal passage schematic. The gateway length, measured footprint, wall junctions and exact position remain unresolved; this is an architectural-form correction, not a registered site plan.

Primary source: [ASI Jaipur Circle, Ranthambhore Fort monument record](https://asijaipurcircle.in/MonumentGallery.aspx?data=70).

## Mandu

UNESCO/ASI describes Hoshang Shah’s Tomb as having a 14.9 m square interior and walls 9.6 m high above the marble platform, with a central dome and four small corner domes. The former 12 m generic footprint was too small even for the stated interior. It is replaced with a 17.3 m outside blockout, 9.6 m wall height and five domes. The exterior wall allowance, arch spacing and marker position are inferred; the source does not provide a registered plan in this record. Mandu remains a fortified city whose plateau boundary, village footprint and water system are not traced in the model. Source: [UNESCO/ASI Mandu description](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/1095/).

## Mehrangarh

Rajasthan Tourism says the fort hill stands 125 m above Jodhpur’s skyline, while the Airports Authority of India gives 150 m for the hill. The current court datum displays about 114 m above its local zero. Because these figures may use different reference points and the local zero is not tied to a surveyed elevation, I left the terrain schematic instead of forcing a potentially misleading number into it. Sources: [Rajasthan Tourism](https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/content/rajasthan-tourism/en/forts.html), [Airports Authority of India](https://aai.aero/en/airports/city-info/jodhpur/Tourist-Place).

## Amer and the Jaipur ridge

The 2023 Rajasthan/JDA eco-sensitive-zone master plan reports a 2022 primary land-use survey for 74.17 ha of Old Amer Special Area, of which 54.11 ha was developed; it lists 19.35 ha residential, 0.97 ha commercial and 2.18 ha mixed use. A new map point surfaces this evidence so Amer’s settlement is represented as a living place in the interface, alongside the palace, water system and Jaigarh connection. These figures do not give parcel outlines or house footprints. The modeled town blocks remain illustrative and are not scaled to the 74.17 ha total. Source: [Rajasthan ESZ master plan](https://environment.rajasthan.gov.in/content/dam/environment/Env/PDF_Files_II/Final%20Report%20Tourism%20Matserplan%20Nahargarh%20ESZ%2003-07-2023.pdf).

## Evidence status and next work

Current aerials help check broad relationships but are not orthophotos with known capture dates. Government map and inventory drawings still need georeferencing against a terrain model. The next reconstruction work should trace the fort and settlement edges, water, gates and approaches, then phase each surviving component against dated plans and images. No site in this pass is marked verified.
