# Rajasthan ridge and river forts: documentary and aerial pass 3

Five playable scenes were checked against official heritage coordinates/maps and current aerial context. This pass improves site anchors and one gateway blockout; it does **not** certify any fort footprint or full 3D reconstruction. All five remain **improved geometry; not verified**.

## What changed

- **Chittorgarh:** Replaced the approximate map anchor with ASI Jodhpur Circle’s coordinate for “Fort of Chitor as a whole” (24.887222 N, 74.646944 E). The large plateau scene and inhabited lower-town cues remain. Current aerial context shows the long north–south mesa directly beside the growing town. The 305 ha UNESCO property boundary is still not aligned to the model.
- **Kumbhalgarh:** Replaced the approximate anchor with ASI’s “Fort of Kumbhalgarh as a whole” coordinate (25.149167 N, 73.581111 E). UNESCO/ASI state that the main wall is about 14 km, and the aerial confirms a broad, forested ridge system. The scene’s wall, drainage and slopes still need geospatial registration.
- **Ranthambore:** Replaced the approximate anchor with UNESCO’s component reference coordinate (26.020278 N, 76.455000 E). Aerial imagery shows an elongated forest ridge, a northward approach and paths extending beyond the fort core. Seven gate names remain sourced, but their scene positions and approach are still schematic.
- **Gagron:** Replaced the approximate anchor with UNESCO’s component reference coordinate (24.626389 N, 76.187222 E). The aerial confirms the fort’s distinctive position by the Ahu–Kali Sindh confluence, with village fabric and fields on the landward side. The scene still uses simplified surrounding channels, so no river course or shoreline is presented as surveyed.
- **Taragarh, Ajmer:** Replaced the generic hill-fort prompt with a documented Lakshmi Pol entrance, based on an ASI scaled plan showing twin bastions and guard rooms. The playable hill area was enlarged to an approximate 80-acre site scale and the present-day scene now includes schematic settlement along the lower rocky ridge. ASI describes a seventh-century fort with nine gates; Rajasthan Tourism attributes the fort to the Chauhans. The chronology and later phases remain unresolved, and only the main gateway is placed.

## Evidence limits

ASI and UNESCO coordinates are reference points for a protected monument or World Heritage component, not full surveyed perimeters. Those points now replace the previous scene markers but do not validate the modeled wall geometry. The UNESCO map lists component area and boundaries; those shapes have not been digitized or transformed into the local scene coordinate system. The ASI Taragarh drawing documents one entrance’s architecture, not the whole fort. Its twin guard-room cues are schematic placeholders based on that drawing, while the other eight gates remain unplaced.

Current satellite views were visually checked for broad terrain, urban and river relationships. Google Maps displayed imagery attribution but not the image acquisition date. No map tiles were traced as measured building data, no elevation surface was registered, and no georeferenced wall or building footprints were created in this pass. The next reconstruction pass needs a registered property plan/orthophoto, DEM-derived slopes and water flow, and dated architectural documentation for every phase.
