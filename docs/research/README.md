# Research system and backlog

Historical and mythological content must be traceable. A convenient game mechanic, generated result, tourism summary, or repeated internet claim is not enough to approve production content.

## Evidence states

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Verified | Supported by appropriate sources, reconciled against meaningful disagreement, and reviewed for the intended use. |
| Supported | Credible evidence exists, but the claim still needs specialist or cross-source review. |
| Provisional | Useful for prototype direction; not safe to present as fact. |
| Disputed | Sources or interpretations materially disagree; the game must represent or explicitly choose among them. |
| Unknown | Research has not answered the question. |
| Design adaptation | Intentionally changed or compressed for play and labelled as such. |

## Source preference

1. Contemporary inscriptions, letters, records, maps, plans, archaeology, surviving objects, and critical editions.
2. Peer-reviewed or university-press scholarship that explains its primary evidence.
3. Official archaeology, museum, archive, gazetteer, and government publications.
4. Specialist secondary works whose evidence can be traced.
5. General summaries only for discovery, never as the sole basis for a precise visual or mechanic.

Older colonial or nationalist scholarship may be valuable but must be read as situated interpretation, not neutral truth. Mythological material requires the text, recension, language, region, date range, and chosen adaptation to be recorded.

## Required record for a production claim

- Claim ID and concise statement.
- Campaign, date/period, place, polity, people, and content category.
- Source IDs plus page, inscription, object, folio, or archive locator.
- Quotation or transcription kept within lawful limits; otherwise a precise paraphrase.
- Evidence state, confidence, conflicting evidence, and reasoning.
- Gameplay or visual use.
- Adaptations made for scale, readability, or narrative.
- Reviewer, review date, and next action.

Use [`research-note-template.md`](research-note-template.md) for a new campaign or topic and register every source in [`source-register.csv`](source-register.csv).

## Research workstreams

- **Chronology and political context:** campaigns, courts, commanders, alliances, disputes, motives, outcomes, and what is genuinely uncertain.
- **Operational geography:** historical coastlines, rivers, passes, roads, ports, forts, settlement networks, travel time, seasons, and weather.
- **Settlement and economy:** occupations, production, storage, trade, taxation, food, water, animals, workshops, administration, and population structure.
- **Forces and command:** recruitment, hierarchy, force composition, training, doctrine, communication, logistics, scouting, morale, and medical support.
- **Material culture:** armour, clothing, weapons, artillery, ships, boats, chariots, elephants, horses, tools, flags, buildings, and everyday objects.
- **Architecture and reconstruction:** measured plans, construction phases, damage/restoration history, defensive function, interiors, sight lines, and landscape relationship.
- **Language and naming:** endonyms, titles, scripts, pronunciation, dialogue register, translation, and historical changes.
- **Mythological traditions:** source traditions, variants, sacred sensitivities, iconography, powers, creatures, cosmology, and explicit adaptation choices.
- **Rights and provenance:** permission, licence, creator, source URL/archive, modification rights, and attribution for every reference and asset.

## Priority backlog for Janjira

1. Build a dated chronology from March–December 1733 with source-level citations.
2. Acquire or create a rights-clear georeferenced base for Janjira, Rajpuri, Dighi, and relevant coastal positions.
3. Separate surviving fort fabric from later repair and reconstruction.
4. Establish documented 1733 Maratha, Angre, Sidi, English, and Portuguese forces and command relationships.
5. Identify vessel types, capacities, propulsion, weapons, crew roles, operational limits, and terminology.
6. Research monsoon sea state, roads, forage, supply, and communication at campaign scale.
7. Validate artillery, infantry, cavalry, clothing, equipment, fort garrison, and camp life.
8. Identify Marathi, Persian, Portuguese, and English primary records and qualified readers/translators.
9. Review how the Siddis and local communities are represented; avoid reducing the campaign to a one-sided heroic narrative.
10. Convert approved findings into content records while retaining citations and adaptation notes.
