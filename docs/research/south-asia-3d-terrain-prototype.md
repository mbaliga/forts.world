# South Asia 3D terrain prototype

This standalone overview builds on the 2026-11-01 handoff and the separate Goa prototype. It does not replace the atlas, local fort scenes, campaign or the Goa terrain. Its rectangular extent is 44–100.5° E and 4.5–39.5° N, covering India and neighbouring regions and extending west into Iran and east into parts of Southeast Asia. This is not a boundary-clipped country map or an assertion that all neighbouring countries are fully covered.

The relief samples the handoff's `strategic-dem-grid.js`: the project averaged mixed-source [Mapzen/Tilezen terrain tiles](https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/) into 0.25° cells, roughly 20–28 km across the region. The source mosaic includes SRTM, GMTED2010 and ETOPO1, with source-dependent datums and resolution; details and tile source records are in `docs/south-asia-strategic-map.md` and `strategic-dem-tile-sources.csv`. The display mesh interpolates this grid; it does not add underlying detail. Heights are exaggerated 15 times. Missing land elevation is gray and rendered at the base plane, not filled with fabricated hills. Because the grid was masked to selected countries, some peripheral areas within the rectangular view lack elevations.

The coastline uses the handoff's simplified Natural Earth 1:10m country polygons, including its India-view political geometry. It is a regional outline, not a detailed or dated shoreline, and small islands, lakes, estuaries and channels can be lost or distorted by the mask and display grid. The optional river overlay uses the handoff's simplified [HydroRIVERS v1.0](https://www.hydrosheds.org/products/hydrorivers) records, displaying Strahler order 6 and above; its modern generalized channels are not historical rivers, fords or route evidence. Colour bands represent elevation and unknown data, not land cover. This overview has no pan-regional vegetation, building-clearance mask, roads or local fort geometry. The separate Goa viewer retains its 2021 land-cover experiment and its own limitations.

The selectable markers are existing project fort catalogue entries within the rectangle, plus Goa prototype additions where not already colocated with a catalogue point. Their positions are approximate research records, not verified fort footprints; they do not constitute all forts. Supplementary Smith–Newton rows remain available in the existing atlas and are not claimed to be included in this 3D overview. The markers do not establish historical phases or a playable site at each position.

Build from the repository root with `node build-south-asia-3d.mjs`; the output is `dist/south-asia-terrain.html`, a self-contained browser file. Tests check dataset shape, sample relief, fort locations, river decoding and the separate Goa data; they do not establish cartographic accuracy or verify visual rendering.

Next work: increase elevation resolution with tiled level-of-detail, add validated coastline and hydrography appropriate to each zoom, acquire appropriately dated and licensed national land-cover and building data, preserve unknown coverage, and connect regional navigation to local scenes. A historical world requires separate period-specific evidence rather than projecting modern data backward.

## Streamed higher-detail view

The separate `south-asia-detail.html` adds continuous zoom and pan using MapLibre, [Mapterhorn's streamed Terrarium terrain tiles](https://mapterhorn.com/) and the [OpenFreeMap Liberty basemap](https://openfreemap.org/quick_start/), derived from OpenStreetMap/OpenMapTiles. Mapterhorn describes its global source as 30 m; source quality, date, elevation type and effective resolution vary and the displayed detail does not constitute a survey. Tiles are 512 pixels with requests capped at zoom 12; at regional scales near zoom 9–10 the terrain tile pixels are approximately 70–150 m across in this region, although mesh resolution, source data and interpolation can be coarser. Terrain heights are exaggerated 1.8 times. The map and terrain need an internet connection; the separate offline overview remains available.

The OSM-based basemap offers more detailed mapped rivers, shorelines, land use and buildings than the earlier regional display, subject to its coverage and errors. It is not a complete current building inventory or an individual-tree model. Fort markers are the existing research catalogue and Goa additions; the Smith–Newton records remain in the atlas. External basemap boundary depictions follow its provider and may differ from the offline handoff's India-view geometry. No historical vegetation, period settlement or fort geometry is implied.

The code and UI are embedded in the output, but basemap styles, fonts, map tiles and elevation tiles are fetched from the respective external services at runtime. Their availability, terms, geographic coverage and changes are outside the project's control. HTTP checks confirmed CORS headers for the tested basemap style and a terrain tile; a mocked integration test checks terrain-source and fort-layer registration. This does not prove all tiles will load or visually validate a scene in the user's browser.

## National-to-local navigation

The detailed viewer overlays a colour-graded image derived from the coarse 0.25° DEM and interpolated contour lines at 500, 1000, 2000, 3000, 4000 and 5000 m. These are regional estimates, not surveyed contour maps; they are hidden where elevation is missing and fade out between zoom levels 6 and 7.5. At closer zoom the streamed modern basemap and elevation take over.

Selecting a fort moves the map to the regional context. Where an existing local scene actually exists, the interface offers an explicit link to it using a validated fort ID; missing scenes show no link. The local scene remains a separate prototype with its own evidence limitations, terrain and scale, not a seamless transition or a faithful completed model. The build determines available scene IDs from the actual level registry. Use the browser's back control to return to the regional viewer.

## Fort catalogue and present-day road correction

The detailed viewer now includes separate project-catalogue and Smith–Newton (2024) supplementary marker layers. Within the bounding box, 3,697 of the paper's 3,863 research rows have numeric coordinates; rows can overlap or duplicate catalogue records and their locations are not independently verified. Rows without usable coordinates remain in the dataset but cannot be placed. The viewer provides a text search over both groups. Selecting an existing local-scene ID opens that scene in a panel within the same page, with a return button; this is an interface transition to a separate scene, not registered or continuous 3D terrain. A research-only record does not acquire a fort model from its marker. This supersedes the preceding paragraph's separate-page navigation description.

The external basemap's transportation and transportation-name layers, airport layers, mapped buildings and residential land-use layer are hidden, to avoid presenting modern roads or buildings as historical. No historical road network is displayed in the regional viewer until independently dated routes have been mapped. Other modern land-cover, water, labels and terrain remain contextual, not period reconstructions. Some separate local research scenes still contain mapped present-day approaches or roads explicitly labeled as such; these need their own date-specific review and are not evidence for an earlier campaign. The external map style could change, so the layer filter must be rechecked against its actual source layers. Automated tests validate layer filtering and panel wiring with a mock, not visual rendering in a browser.
