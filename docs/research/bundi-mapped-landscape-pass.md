# Bundi: mapped fort and town landmarks

The previous local origin and discovery marker at 25.444°N, 75.637°E lay roughly 700 m south of the mapped Taragarh wall. The research scene and catalogue marker now use an approximate fort-area origin at 25.451°N, 75.6375°E. It is a local reference, not a surveyed control point. Earlier inferred fort, town, palace and water placements were not registered to this frame and are superseded.

The present scene uses the following OpenStreetMap outlines, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL:

- [Taragarh wall way 1547844077](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1547844077), an open mapped line, not necessarily the full fort circuit. Another mapped fort way overlaps parts of the area; their relationship needs survey.
- [Garh Palace relation 21195506](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/21195506), outer precinct only; its courtyard and building architecture are not reconstructed.
- [Nawal Sagar](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/42038601) and [Jait Sagar](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/42043168) present shoreline polygons.
- [Raniji ki Baori](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/42223788), [Sukh Mahal](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1547844034), and [Garh Palace Road](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/42038640) as present mapped footprints or centre line.

The stepwell uses the [Rajasthan Tourism-reported 46 m depth](https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/raniji-ki-baori.html), converted to the scene's 4:1 vertical compression. Its mapped outline is the site footprint, not a surveyed excavation or stair plan; five stairs, water level and interior are illustrative. Sukh Mahal is a generic block on a mapped outline. The lake floor and water levels are chosen from a coarse terrain model, not water surveys; vegetation, water seasonality, the lake temple and nearby urban fabric are not reconstructed.

Terrain uses 121 [SRTM30 samples via OpenTopodata](https://www.opentopodata.org/datasets/srtm/), retrieved 1 November 2026 at 400 m spacing across 4 × 4 km. This supports broad spatial relationships only; it cannot resolve the ridge, wall heights, palace levels, stepwells or lake surfaces. Land outside the sample coverage blends toward a placeholder. The 200 m elevation reference and 4:1 vertical compression produce approximate above-sea-level readouts, not surveyed elevations.

Only the present-day research scene is offered: present mapped outlines do not establish the 1400, 1700 or 1800 state. This does not imply the documented earlier sites were absent. Dated plans, detailed terrain, current condition imagery, measured palace and stepwell drawings, and mapped settlement are still required. The [2024 Rajasthan urban water report](https://www.lsg.urban.rajasthan.gov.in/content/dam/raj/udh/organizations/ruidp/SafeguardPolicies/IEEMarch-2024/Bundi%20Water%20Supply%20and%20Wastewater%20Works%20IEE.pdf) supplies useful city and monument context but is not a geometrically registered fort survey here. Automated reachability does not establish fidelity.
