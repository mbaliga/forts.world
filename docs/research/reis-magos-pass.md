# Reis Magos: fort-fidelity pass (batch 1)

Date of pass: 2026-11-04. Branch `fort-fidelity`. Trace: `src/levels/reis-magos.trace.js`
(from `data/traces/reis-magos.trace.json` plus `tools/trace/curation/reis-magos.mjs`). Level: `src/levels/reis-magos.js`.
The previous hand level is kept as `src/levels/reis-magos-hand.js` (id `reis-magos-hand`, not registered in `index.js`) so its points of interest can be folded into a T2 trace.
Builds on `reis-magos-ground-truth-pass.md`; its caveats still stand unless corrected below.

**Method and limit.** Text only. Wikipedia and the Goa Government page were read this pass; Lopes fig. 7, Kiperman pp. 183-184 and the on-site plan photograph
were **not opened** (they are cited from the earlier pass). No plan or photograph was viewed, so nothing here reaches T2.

## What changed from the hand level, and why

The hand level drew things no source places: a gate at the "junction of the two levels", a stair between levels, two lower pavilions, a church mass of 8 m, a 20 m
church stair, 4 m walls 1.7 m thick, a spring marker and a 33-gun statement. Several were labelled "approximate" or "provisional", but they were still drawn as solid objects,
which is why this fort was judged the weakest. The new level keeps only mapped lines and documented dates. It is therefore **plainer** than the hand level, deliberately.
Those placements are in the hand file for T2.

## Inferred (flagged `inferred` in the trace)

- **Historic eras.** The present perimeter, coast and sea are carried back to 1551 and 1707; the church footprint to 1707. The 1551 extent may differ.
- **Heights.** None; everything is a kerb-high plan line.

## Not drawn, although text describes it

Gate (the hand level's position came from eye comparison with Lopes fig. 7), internal and church stairs, lower pavilions, Hall 2 and the craft centre, turrets, the 33 guns,
the spring (sources disagree on east of the fort or within it), tunnels, the restoration exhibit and model.

## Conflicts

- Origin: 1493 (Adil Shah outpost) versus 1551 (Portuguese erection).
- Jail: from 1900, or from after the British left.
- Spring: east of the fort or inside it.
- Wikipedia calls the fort "in a good state of preservation" yet crumbling after 1993.
- The Goa Government page still says the fort is not open to the public; the on-site photographs show a restored, visited site.

## Missing evidence for T2
