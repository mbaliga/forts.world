# Maharashtra plateau forts: documentary pass 9

This five-fort pass covers Lohagad, Paranda, Ahmednagar, Udgir and Kandhar. It corrects several scene-type mismatches and adds documented counts, chronology and site relationships. These remain playable research blockouts, **not verified reconstructions**. I consulted government tourism and district descriptions and the Maharashtra State Gazetteers; no fort-scale measured drawing was registered, and I did not visually inspect current aerial pixels in this pass.

## Changes

- **Lohagad:** The State Gazetteer describes a Sahyadri summit plateau, outer and inner defenses, four formidable gateways in succession, cisterns, the Lohavadi hamlet below the escarpment, and the 1648/1665/1670 control sequence. The scene now distinguishes the final Maha Darwaja from the four-gate sequence, adds a schematic Vinchukata spur wall and labels the southern Jain-cave cue. The route, spur alignment, summit perimeter, gate positions and settlement remain untraced. The modern state-tourism page gives a different early chronology than the older gazetteer, so the deeper origin remains unresolved. [Maharashtra Tourism: Lohagad](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/fort/lohagad/), [Poona District Gazetteer: Lohagad](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Poona/PART%20VI/Chap%20%2820%29/Lohagad-2.html)
- **Paranda:** Removed the false hill terrain and changed the prototype to a flatland, moat-fort blockout. The Maharashtra State Gazetteer records 26 rounded bastions, two flanking a northern entrance, a moat and bridge, guard rooms and a mosque; the moat is described as mostly silted. The scene distributes 26 bastion cues around a schematic enclosure and adds a mosque mass. The enclosure shape, water, bastion positions and mosque footprint are inferred. The attribution to Mahmud Gawan and several control dates conflict across historical summaries and need primary-source resolution. [Dharashiv District: Paranda Fort](https://dharashiv.maharashtra.gov.in/tourist-place/paranda-fort/), [Maharashtra State Gazetteer: Nizamshahi chapter](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/History%20Part/History_II/chapter_3.pdf)
- **Ahmednagar:** Replaced the nearby-river scene with an oval moated-fort blockout. Written descriptions report a 1.7 km circumference, 24 bastions, a moat and historic east/west entrances; other government tourism copy gives a much larger, inconsistent extent, so the scene uses the smaller figure only as a provisional scale constraint, not a survey measurement. Notes separate the earlier 1490 earthwork from the 1553–65 stone rebuilding. The palace and 1942–45 detention context are labeled; their footprints and the 1943 entrance alteration remain unmapped. [Maharashtra Tourism: Ahmednagar](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/fort/ahmednagar/), [India Cine Hub, Government of India: Ahmednagar Fort](https://indiacinehub.gov.in/location/ahmednagar-fort), [Maharashtra Gazetteer: Ahmednagar](https://www.gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Ahmadnagar/places_Ahmadnagar.html)
- **Udgir:** Removed the isolated hill-fort terrain because the official description places the fort within Udgir city and describes a deep surrounding trench, palaces and Udgir Buva’s samadhi about 60 feet below ground. The scene now uses a level urban-fort blockout, with a schematic trench and a vertical access cue to the samadhi. The claimed 12th-century date, exact trench geometry, the samadhi plan and the town relationship need corroboration and survey. A rumored connection tunnel is deliberately not represented as fact. [Latur District: Udgir Fort](https://latur.gov.in/en/tourist-place/udagir-fort/), [Osmanabad District Gazetteer: places](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Osmanabad/places.html)
- **Kandhar:** Replaced the river-shore prototype with a flatland moat-fort scene. Nanded District records an approximately 24-acre extent, a moat roughly 100 feet wide and 25 feet deep, two defensive lines, 32 bastions, four entrances, and a list of palace, administrative, religious and storage structures. The model now constrains its broad ellipse to that approximate area, adds a second defensive circuit and 32 bastion cues, and labels several named structures and the Ambari Cannon. All positions and the shape of both circuits remain schematic. [Nanded District: Kandhar Bhuikot Fort](https://nanded.gov.in/en/tourist-place/kandhar-bhuikot-fort/), [Maharashtra Tourism: Kandhar](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/fort/kandhar/)

## Verification gaps

None of the five has a registered wall trace, site-scale official plan, dated photo set or current orthophoto comparison in this pass. Government prose supplied useful relative layout clues, counts and chronology, but did not validate the new coordinates. The next reconstruction stage needs scaled fort plans, official conservation/archaeological drawings, current imagery and a terrain model; then each period must be checked against dated fabric and ownership history.
