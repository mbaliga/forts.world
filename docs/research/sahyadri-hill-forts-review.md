# Sahyadri passes and hill forts: five-site evidence review

Review date: 31 October 2026

This pass covers Torna, Sinhagad, Shivneri, Purandar and Panhala as a connected set of Sahyadri routes and upland settlements. Catalogue coordinates remain approximate discovery references. All five scenes are playable blockouts; none is a verified reconstruction or a site-specific faithful model at this point.

## Torna / Prachandagad

Maharashtra Tourism identifies Torna's high Sahyadri setting, Velhe access, Bini and Kothi gates, Zunjar and Budhla machis, reservoirs, and the broad sequence from a reported 13th-century origin through Bijapur, Maratha, Mughal and Maratha control. The Poona Gazetteer provides older location/elevation descriptions and historic context. The current scene does not trace the irregular mountain-top footprint, cliffs, machis, routes or water features from mapped evidence. The next pass should obtain a scaled site plan and DEM, then register the ridge, gate approaches and tank locations before replacing the generic outline.

References: [Maharashtra Tourism: Torna](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/fort/torna/); [Poona District Gazetteer, places of interest](https://www.gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Poona/PART%20VI/Chap%20%2820%29/Places%20Of%20Interest.htm).

## Sinhagad / Kondhana

The Poona Gazetteer describes a nearly triangular walled plateau about two miles around, two principal gates on the north-east and south-east, and steep winding ascents. Maharashtra Tourism summarizes the 1670 battle. The scene does not use a registered outline or reconstruct the two gate routes and cliff faces. Next, find and digitize a measured/ASI plan, then register it against a DEM and current aerial; distinguish permanent fort fabric and later memorial/visitor structures by date.

References: [Poona District Gazetteer history chapter](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/History%20Part/History_II/chapter_3.pdf); [Maharashtra Tourism: Sinhagad](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/fort/sinhagad/).

## Shivneri

UNESCO's nomination dossier records the Shivneri component property area, coordinate and four indexed map sheets, with stated scales of 1:15,000 and 1:30,000. These are valuable spatial references, but the sheets have not yet been digitized or fitted to the game scene. The Government of Maharashtra tourism page supplies a narrative overview. The current generic scene therefore cannot yet support faithful period-specific reconstructions. Next retrieve the indexed sheets, map the fort gates and approach sequence, and connect Shivneri to Junnar town, nearby caves and the surrounding pass network.

References: [UNESCO component map/index](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1739/maps/); [India's UNESCO nomination dossier](https://whc.unesco.org/document/206086); [Maharashtra Tourism: Shivneri](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/fort/shivneri/).

## Purandar

Maharashtra Tourism gives the Treaty of Purandar and the siege as key regional episodes, but this pass found no measured plan or registered aerial for the scene. The current model is a generated blockout and does not distinguish Purandar from the adjacent Vajragad, or encode the connecting saddle and approach roads. Next gather an authoritative plan and current terrain data, then model the paired heights and route network before dating and placing individual buildings.

Reference: [Maharashtra Tourism: Purandar](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/fort/purandar/).

## Panhala

UNESCO records the component coordinate and 181.02 ha nominated property area; that boundary is not the fort-wall footprint. ASI's protected-monument list names key features, including Teen Darwaja, Wagh Darwaja, the Ambarkhana granaries, Andhar Bavdi, Dharma Kothi and Naikinicha Sajja. Maharashtra Tourism's 2025 booklet describes the plateau/pass setting, long fortifications and broad Shilahara and Maratha history. The current scene is an oval parametric blockout with hand-placed structures, not a traced plan, and does not reproduce the living hill town. Next digitize the UNESCO map sheets and ASI feature records, register the fort-town boundary and pass routes, then phase each named structure.

References: [UNESCO component map/index](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1739/maps/); [UNESCO nomination dossier](https://whc.unesco.org/document/206086); [ASI protected monuments list](https://asi.nic.in/admin/whatsnew/download/585); [Maharashtra Tourism forts booklet, p. 19](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Maharashtra-Booklet_compressed.pdf).

## Shared regional work

The UNESCO dossier defines these forts as a connected defense landscape in which terrain and adjacent environmental features matter. The present game has no registered regional terrain, pass, road or village network joining these five sites. Build one Sahyadri terrain reference and trace inter-site routes and seasonal constraints before treating the scenes as campaign-ready geography. Do not use UNESCO component polygons as fort walls or backdate current structures into earlier periods.
