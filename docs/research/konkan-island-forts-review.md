# Konkan and Deccan fort review: five connected sites

Review date: 31 October 2026

This pass preserves the existing catalogue coordinates as approximate discovery points. It distinguishes a playable scene from an evidence-registered reconstruction. None of the five scenes qualifies as verified after this review.

## Murud-Janjira

The existing Janjira scene is a detailed custom island-fort model with a traced wall loop, gates, water features and a Siddi/Maratha siege scenario. The catalogue includes a 1774 plan and historic photographs, and Maharashtra Tourism describes the oval rock, bastions, the concealed Rajapuri-facing entrance and boat access. Those sources do not establish a georeferenced wall trace or surveyed current footprint. The tourism page also gives inconsistent foundation chronology. Preserve the current geometry as a researched blockout; next align the 1774 plan and trace to a dated aerial, then test each major interior feature and phase. Do not treat the 1774 drawing as a present-day footprint without accounting for change.

References: [Maharashtra Tourism: Murud-Janjira](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/fort/murud-janjira/); [historic plan catalogue, BnF/Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/en/item/9200517/ark__12148_btv1b85931692); [Pushkar Sohoni, “The Fort of Janjira”](https://www.academia.edu/44679159).

## Sindhudurg

The custom scene uses an estimated island outline roughly 705 by 375 m, rotated 20 degrees, with a 40-bastion wall and hand-placed interior features. The 19.5 ha area is close to UNESCO's 19.11 ha inscribed-property area, but the latter is a heritage-property boundary, not proof of the wall trace. Maharashtra Tourism reports 48 acres, 30-foot walls and 12-foot thickness; these claims are not independent survey measurements. The government page supports a 1664–67 construction account, the hidden entrance, freshwater sources, temple and seasonal boat access. Use the UNESCO coordinate only as a reference point, not as a wall vertex. Next register the historic plan against current imagery, check orientation and bastion count, and add reef/bathymetry and seasonal access from authoritative hydrographic data.

References: [UNESCO component map and data](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1739/maps/); [Maharashtra Tourism: Sindhudurg](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/fort/sindhudurg/); [Government of Maharashtra tourism master plan, proposed Sindhudurg zones and trails](https://sd2.tourism.gov.in/DocumentRepoFiles/MasterPlan/MP451c28ab-823a-4e8e-a493-cc52040d86ab.pdf).

## Daulatabad / Devagiri

The scene already encodes a conical citadel, scarp, moat, layered defenses and named monuments, but several measures and placements remain schematic. A University of Hyderabad repository dissertation contains a site plan with north arrow, a 0–200 m scale, and numbered locations for the eastern gate, bazaar, Hathi Haud, Jami Mosque, Chand Minar, Mughal Palace, Chini Mahal, Bahmani Palace, artificial caves and Mughal baradari. It describes the successive Yadava, Khalji, Tughluq, Bahmani, Nizam Shahi and Mughal contributions. This is a useful scaled plan, not yet a georeferenced survey. The next pass should digitize its layers and fit them to current aerial/topographic data, preserving the distinction between the fort and its extensive historic town.

Reference: [University of Hyderabad repository dissertation, “Daulatabad – Site Plan”](https://igmlnet.uohyd.ac.in/docs/hi-res/hcu_images/TH13513.pdf), especially printed pp. 63–65 (PDF pages 64–66).

## Golconda

The existing scene describes its layout as a design estimate because no measured plan had been found. The Deccan study adds historic plan plates and phase context, but they have not been scaled, transcribed or aligned to current aerials. Consequently the model's outer city, Bala Hisar hill, routes and internal buildings remain unverified. Next digitize plan plates with scale control, cross-check with ASI documentation and a current terrain/aerial base, then date individual architectural elements before enabling earlier campaign snapshots.

References: [Sardar, “Golconda through time”](https://the-deccan.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Sardar_Golconda_through_time.pdf); [ASI centrally protected monuments list, Hyderabad Circle](https://asi.nic.in/pdf/CPM_List.pdf).

## Vijaydurg

Vijaydurg is presently served by the shared generic Maratha playable blockout, not a site-specific 3D reconstruction. UNESCO gives a 9.27 ha component property and a reference coordinate; that area must not be substituted for the fort-wall footprint. Maharashtra Tourism and the Konkan Divisional Commissioner provide starting points for history and setting, but the reported early origins, tunnel and other particular claims need corroboration. No verified plan or current aerial registration is part of the scene. This site should receive its own model after obtaining an authoritative plan and terrain/shoreline data, with the fort, Waghotan creek, harbour approaches and living settlement represented as connected layers.

References: [UNESCO component map and data](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1739/maps/); [Maharashtra Tourism: Vijaydurg](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/fort/vijaydurg/); [Konkan Divisional Commissioner: Vijaydurg](https://divcomkonkan.gov.in/en/places-centres/vijaydurg/).

## Status and next evidence gate

This is an evidence and prioritization pass, not a claim of completion. The Janjira and Sindhudurg scenes have substantial custom geometry; Daulatabad and Golconda have playable scenes with significant unregistered plan work remaining; Vijaydurg is still generic. All five remain `NOT VERIFIED` in the machine-generated fidelity audit. A site can advance only when its plan/footprint is registered to an authoritative spatial base, dated phases and ownership are tied to sources, and settlement, access and current condition are checked independently.
