# Western-coast and southern forts: geometry pass 8

**Reis Magos correction:** The Reis Magos description below records a superseded earlier pass. The generic oval, invented gun placements, church block and spring marker were removed after the scaled aerial plan and mapped outline were reviewed. See [the later correction](reis-magos-ground-truth-pass.md) for the current scene and its remaining limits. The 1710 scene is no longer offered.

**Chapora correction:** Its earlier oval, guessed tunnel passages and tombstone placements described below were replaced by a mapped perimeter or removed. See [the Chapora correction](chapora-mapped-perimeter-pass.md); the 1730 scene is no longer offered pending dated architectural evidence.

This pass covers Reis Magos, Chapora, Mirjan, St Angelo’s Fort and Thirumayam. It adds documentary features to the playable scenes and improves a few relative relationships. **All five remain not verified.** The official tourism and district pages are useful written evidence, but they do not provide georeferenced fort plans or phase-specific measured drawings. I did not inspect current aerial imagery pixel-by-pixel during this pass, so no aerial comparison is claimed.

## Changes and evidence

- **Reis Magos, Goa:** Government of Goa describes a fort above the Mandovi estuary, founded in 1551 and rebuilt in 1707, with 33 guns, a spring to the east and the church below. The c. 1710 scene now includes 33 historic gun cues, an eastern spring marker and a church mass at the hill foot. Their local positions and shapes are explicitly schematic; the estuary shoreline and fort outline remain unregistered. [Government of Goa: Reis Magos Fort](https://www.goa.gov.in/places/reis-magos-fort/)
- **Chapora, Goa:** Government of Goa records the Portuguese return in 1717, two surviving tunnel heads and tombstones on the southern slope, as well as changes of control in 1684, 1717, 1739 and 1741. The period and present scenes now show two south-slope portal cues; the present scene also has tombstone markers. Tunnel routes, construction sequence and feature coordinates are unknown, so the portals are explicitly schematic. [Government of Goa: Chapora Fort](https://www.goa.gov.in/places/chapora-fort/)
- **Mirjan, Karnataka:** Karnataka Tourism describes four principal entrances, water-filled moats and canals, wells, and an approximately 10-acre extent; its account also mentions an audience hall. The scene now constrains its oval area to approximately 10 acres at the existing 3 m horizontal scale, includes four entrance cues, four well markers and an audience-hall mass. No gate positions, perimeter geometry or channel courses have been traced. The date and builder attribution remain provisional because the available official accounts differ. [Karnataka Tourism: Mirjan Fort](https://old.karnatakatourism.org/tour-item/mirjan-fort/)
- **St Angelo’s Fort, Kannur:** Kerala Tourism describes a triangular laterite fort with three bastions and, in its site history, a north-east gateway, jail in the northern rampart, east–west stables, chapel, ammunition depot, lighthouse, sea wall, tombstones and artillery. The present scene now exposes labeled blockouts for these features and aims its entrance north-east. Building footprints, bastion placements and sea-wall course are inferred; the early Portuguese phase does not assume that present-day ancillary buildings already existed. [Kerala Tourism: Kannur Fort history](https://www.keralatourism.org/kerala-article/2020/kannur-fort-where-history-sleeps/1019)
- **Thirumayam, Tamil Nadu:** Pudukkottai District describes a 1687 fort, says the historic extent was about twice the visible remains, places its main entrance one kilometre from the present structure, and lists rock-cut Shiva and Vishnu temples, Darbar Hall and a pillared corridor at the hill foot. The playable hill envelope now reflects the stated approximate doubled area; hill-foot cave-mouth, hall and corridor blockouts make the documented complex explorable. This is an area constraint, not a reconstructed wall line. The remote entrance’s direction and all component footprints remain unknown. [Pudukkottai District: Thirumayam Fort](https://pudukkottai.nic.in/tourist-place/thirumayam-fort/)

## Verification state and next evidence needed

These are evidence-informed interactive blockouts, not faithful finished models. The source pages supply counts, chronology, broad placement and some orientation clues. For each site, the next pass should acquire or inspect a site plan, archaeological or conservation drawing, dated photographs and a current orthophoto/aerial; register them to a common coordinate frame; trace the wall, terrain, water and structures; then compare each dated scene with its correct construction and ownership phase. Catalogue coordinates remain approximate discovery coordinates and have not been used as surveyed building control points.

The build’s plan smoke checks pass for both available eras at all five sites. This confirms that the scenes load and their navigation graph is connected; it does not validate historical geometry.
