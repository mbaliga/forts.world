# Revdanda: mapped fortified-town baseline

The previous scene was a generic fort blockout and put the approximate marker northeast of the mapped fort. The present scene uses 18.5474°N, 72.9268°E as an approximate projection origin, not a measured monument coordinate.

The wall follows [OpenStreetMap way 658563498](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/658563498), tagged as Revdanda Fort and a city wall; its extent is a mapping trace, not a measured fortification plan. [Coastline way 620766380](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/620766380) and a section of [modern road 102722845](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/102722845) are mapped in the same frame. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. The coastline is clipped and artificially closed on three edges; the closure is not a measured shore. The road is a modern centreline, not proof of a historical street.

The [Kolaba Gazetteer](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/KOLABA/places_Cheul.html) describes a Portuguese fortified town, including churches, walls, gateways and a northern moat. Those structures are not located by this pass. The wall is opened at two intersections of the mapped fort polygon with [OSM road 104841417](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/104841417). The openings are each an illustrative 9 m across along the wall. Their exact width, architecture and identity with historic gates are unverified. The other mapped highway runs outside the polygon and is not used to infer an opening. Wall thickness and height are placeholders. No churches, town buildings, moat, or historical construction phases are reconstructed.

The terrain uses land-masked [SRTM30](https://www.opentopodata.org/datasets/srtm/) samples at 140 m spacing acquired 1 November 2026. Its shoreline, water level, creek bed and urban topography are too coarse for a faithful fort scene. Korlai and the opposite bank are outside this local scene. The previous c.1610 state was removed; a present outline does not establish that period’s structures or town. Measured plans, dated imagery and surveys of the walls, living settlement, churches, moat and estuary remain necessary.


The Gazetteer describes a road from the sea gate through the enclosure to the north gate. The two mapped intersections are consistent with a through-road but do not, by themselves, prove which surviving gateways are depicted, or the road’s earlier alignment. Navigation testing confirms both exterior road points are reachable from inside in the present scene; this is a gameplay check, not historical verification.
