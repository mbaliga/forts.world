# Malwa urban heritage, Deccan fort-capital and northwest corridors

**Status:** research and map-layer pass; not architectural reconstruction. Source review: 1 November 2026.

## Why these belong in IN-RTS

Fort locations alone cannot explain how armies, state power, trade and local communities moved through a region. This pass adds map views for route corridors, fortified cities, palace districts and water systems. Heritage and campaign-place markers remain separate from fort inventory records. Point anchors are not wall plans or tactical navigation geometry.

## Northwest: Silk Roads were plural

UNESCO describes Bamiyan as a stopping place on a branch of the Silk Route linking China and India via ancient Bactria. Its tentative-list dossier for Indian Silk Road sites discusses several possible connections through the northwest, including routes associated with Taxila/Gandhara and with the Afghanistan highlands. UNESCO’s broader overview explicitly warns that the routes did not follow a single itinerary. The project therefore adds a **Northwest corridors** evidence view around the existing Taxila and Bamiyan heritage markers, but does not draw a single definitive Silk Road polyline. Future route layers should be dated and branched, with passes and source-backed nodes, instead of presenting a modern simplified itinerary as the one ancient road.

Sources: [UNESCO Bamiyan Valley](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/208/); [UNESCO tentative list: Silk Road Sites in India](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5492/); [UNESCO: About the Silk Roads](https://www.unesco.org/en/silk-roads/about-silk-roads).

This follow-up adds **Bala Hisar mound at Charsadda**, identified by Pakistan's Department of Archaeology and Museums with ancient Pushkalavati, a former Gandhara capital. The same official record gives a site coordinate, records 52 cultural layers from the 6th century BCE to the 16th century CE, and describes resistance during Alexander's campaign in 327 BCE. It is added to the cultural/campaign layer, not the fort count: the coordinate identifies Bala Hisar, while Pushkalavati was a wider multi-mound settlement. The northwest map view now names Taxila, Pushkalavati and Bamiyan and summarizes the branches proposed by UNESCO's India tentative-list dossier, while continuing to avoid a single unsupported route trace.

Source: [Pakistan Department of Archaeology and Museums: Bala Hisar Mound](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/2160).

## Bhopal: fort, city walls, palace and water system

This pass separates the Bhopal material into an approximate Fatehgarh/old-city fortification anchor, a more closely located Islamnagar fort wall and palace group, and a heritage marker for Rani Kamlapati Mahal and its hydraulic setting.

The National Monuments Authority’s draft conservation plan for Rani Kamlapati Palace describes an adjoining **panchakki (water mill)** and **baoli**, supplied by water brought from Upper Lake through a tunnel. This supports the user’s recollection of the water mill, with an important spatial refinement: the mill is in the palace’s adjoining structure, rather than an independent mill sitting between the two lakes. The palace itself lies on the embankment between Upper and Lower Lakes. The project uses the palace’s approximate coordinate for the complex; it does not invent a separate mill coordinate or tunnel line.

The Madhya Pradesh draft development plan describes Fatehgarh Fort, the later fortified Shahjahanabad extension, historic gates and remaining urban morphology. The Imperial Gazetteer describes a wall enclosing the Shahr-i-Khas. Current urban reuse of surviving fabric is a strong investigation lead, but this pass does not claim a traced surviving perimeter or assign specific current government offices to historic walls without a building-level record.

The planning source names the weekday gates Somwara, Budhwara, Itwara and Jumerati; UN-Habitat's Bhopal city profile counts 14 historic gates of the fortified city and notes the old walled city in the modern urban sequence. These names and count are now shown in the Bhopal–Indore map view. They remain attached to an old-city context point because this pass has not registered individual gate coordinates from the plan figure. UN-Habitat also describes Bhopal's hilly tableland, the old city between Idgah and Shamla hills along Upper Lake, southeastward slope, and drainage via Halali and Kaliyasot to the Betwa. These modern geographic relations can guide later route-cost and watershed work; they do not establish historical lake levels or seasonal crossing conditions.

The CGWB National Aquifer Mapping report for Bhopal lists 13 water-body point coordinates and paired pre-2010/2019 water-spread areas. The map now exposes these as a separate optional blue-marker layer, retaining the report's values and coordinate precision. These are point anchors, not digitized shorelines; the paired areas show change and must not be treated as fixed historical footprints. Two tank names in the report share the same coordinate, so both entries are preserved as recorded. CGWB describes the Kaliasot Reservoir as receiving Upper Lake spill through Bhadbhada gate. MPPCB traces the Kaliasot downstream to the Betwa near Bhojpur and notes seasonal low flow, including a lower reach that can be dry for much of the year. Bhopal Development Plan 1991 records a separate northeast drainage relation through Patra Nallah into the Halali system and onward to the Betwa downstream of Vidisha. The map presents these as a sourced relationship schematic only; no new river polyline or watershed boundary is claimed.

Sources: [CGWB, National Aquifer Mapping report for Bhopal](https://cgwb.gov.in/cgwbpnm/public/uploads/documents/17514136921720986469file.pdf); [MPPCB, Action Plan for Rejuvenation of River Kaliasot](https://mppcb.mp.gov.in/proc/KALIYASOTE%2023.5.19.pdf); [Bhopal Master Plan 1991](https://www.mptownplan.gov.in/LU-panel/Bhopal/Bhopal%20Master%20Plan%201991%20English.pdf).

Sources: [National Monuments Authority: Rani Kamlapati Palace conservation plan](https://www.nma.gov.in/showfile.php?lang=1&level=1&lid=2203&ls_id=1766); [Bhopal Development Plan 2031](https://mptownplan.gov.in/LU-panel/Bhopal/Amrut/ENGLISH/VOL1.pdf); [UN-Habitat India: Bhopal City Profile and Diagnostic Report (2021), topography and built-heritage sections](https://unhabitat.org.in/sites/default/files/2024-11/city_profile_and_diagnostic_report_bhopal_compressed_compressed.pdf); [Imperial Gazetteer: Bhopal city](https://library.bjp.org/jspui/bitstream/123456789/1941/1/The-Imperial-Gazetteer-Of-India-Vol-8%28Berhampore%20to%20Bombay%29.pdf); [Bhopal district history](https://bhopal.nic.in/en/history/).

## Indore: Rajwada, later palace and memorial landscape

Rajwada is already classified in the new heritage context layer as a **fortified palace**: the Indore District describes four cylindrical corner bastions. Its entry is not counted as a separate fort because the evidence supports a fortified dynastic residence and urban centre, not a conventional detached fort. Lalbagh Palace is added as a distinct later Holkar residence; Krishnapura Chhatris as a memorial landscape along the Khan River. The map view connects these places without implying one continuous fort enclosure.

The Kamla Nehru Prani Sangrahalaya at Navlakha is documented by the Central Zoo Authority as a municipal zoological garden. The sources reviewed here do not substantiate a former fort at the zoo site, so it is not mapped as a fort. Local oral history or older survey maps could reopen the question.

Sources: [Indore District: Rajwada](https://indore.nic.in/en/tourist-place/rajwada/); [Indore District: Lalbagh Palace](https://indore.nic.in/en/tourist-place/lalbag-palace/); [Madhya Pradesh Tourism: Indore heritage walk](https://www.mptourism.com/heritage-walk-in-indore.html); [Central Zoo Authority: Indore Zoo report](https://cza.nic.in/uploads/documents/reports/english/AR_indorezoo_2122.pdf).

## Bengaluru: yes, Bangalore Fort is already present

The fort catalogue contains Bangalore Fort and the playable level list includes its scene. Bengaluru Urban district history identifies the 1537 Kempe Gowda mud fort as foundational to the city. The current point is an approximate fort anchor; the early mud fort footprint and its later rebuildings need more exact map evidence. The map now has a focused view for this record and its later Mysore/colonial military context.

Sources: [Bengaluru Urban District history](https://bengaluruurban.nic.in/en/history/); [Bengaluru Urban District tourism](https://bengaluruurban.nic.in/en/tourism/); [Karnataka Tourism: Bengaluru](https://karnatakatourism.org/en/destinations/bengaluru/).

## Hyderabad: existing fort anchors, larger complex still to map

The fort catalogue already includes Golconda, with a playable fort scene, and Bhongir. Telangana’s official district description calls Golconda a large fortress complex and gives an outer enclosure of about 3 km². The new view identifies Naya Qila as an annex of Golconda, not an unrelated fort, and separates the nearby Qutb Shahi Tombs and Charminar from the fort count. Charminar is a city monument. The Qutb Shahi group and later Asaf Jahi palaces provide wider context. City walls, gates, outlying defensive works, water infrastructure and date-specific street growth remain important gaps for source-led mapping.

Sources: [Hyderabad District: Golconda Fort](https://hyderabad.telangana.gov.in/tourist-place/golconda-fort/); [UNESCO tentative-list dossier: Qutb Shahi Monuments](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5573/); [Comptroller and Auditor General: Golconda/Naya Qila protection record](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2013/Union_Performance_Ministry_Cultures_Monuments_Antiquities_18_2013_chapter_12.pdf); [Telangana Tourism: heritage sites](https://www.tourism.telangana.gov.in/customized-heritage-sites-monuments).

## What changed in the map

- Added an evidence view for northwest Silk Road corridors, with multi-route wording and no unsupported route line.
- Added Bhopal/Indore, Hyderabad and Bengaluru regional evidence views.
- Added heritage markers for Kamlapati Palace and associated panchakki/water system, Bhopal urban fortifications, Islamnagar, Rajwada, Lalbagh Palace, Krishnapura Chhatris and the wider Qutb Shahi context.
- Added the official-coordinate Bala Hisar/Pushkalavati campaign-settlement marker and updated the northwest corridor view to include it.
- Added Fatehgarh and Islamnagar fortification records with caveated positions; no new tactical or 3D scenes were inferred from point records.
- Consolidated map-object controls into a collapsible layer panel: curated forts, supplementary records, OSM fort points, towns/ports/passes, heritage sites, scenario links, Bhopal water points, rivers, boundaries, labels and coverage gaps can be toggled independently. Country/state filters and name search remain available.
- Added elevation-band and satellite-inspired shaded-relief render styles for the same imported modern DEM. This changes appearance only; it is not satellite imagery and is not a historical surface reconstruction. Campaign year continues to filter only date-bounded campaign places.

## Next evidence work

1. Georeference historical Bhopal plans and record Fatehgarh/Shahjahanabad wall lines, gates, bastions, reused sections and office occupants separately by date.
2. Locate the Kamlapati panchakki, baoli and water tunnel independently from the palace point, and determine their extent from the NMA plan and field evidence.
3. Compare the early Kempe Gowda fort outline with Mysore-era and British-period Bengaluru plans; register surviving fragments and gates.
4. Build a dated Hyderabad fort-capital layer for Golconda, Naya Qila, defensive walls and gates, Charminar/Hyderabad, linked waterworks, and the siege/urban growth episodes.
5. Build Silk Roads as a set of dated branch hypotheses backed by specific itineraries, archaeology and pass evidence; keep them distinct from the later Delhi–Kandahar Mughal scroll route.
6. A period-specific satellite-like layer still needs dated evidence for settlement, vegetation, coastlines, water and land use. Deep-time scenarios (including Last Glacial Maximum shorelines or pre-Suez geography) require paleogeographic bathymetry and sea-level inputs; current modern DEM and river data cannot supply those changes by themselves.
