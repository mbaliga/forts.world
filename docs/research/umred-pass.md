## Documented in text (Durgbharari, not placed)
- "Believed" built in the 16th century by the Gond king Karanshah of Chanda; later held by the Bhosles of Nagpur; British rule from 1850. No external source is cited for the origin.
- Brick ramparts with five bastions with gun openings. Bastion 1 holds the tomb of Hazrat Baba Chandshah Wali; bastion 3 is on school grounds; bastion 4 in a municipal garden; bastion 5 has an old Shiv temple, a well, a prayer hall and a Hanuman idol. Only one side of the ramparts and the five bastions survive; the original size cannot be estimated.

## Inferred
- The 16th-century era is the mapped strip carried back, marked inferred. No bastion, gate or wall height is drawn.

## Conflicts and doubts
- The OSM strip is named as the fort but is 199 x 58 m, far smaller than a fort with a lake "inside" it; it is the surviving rampart side, not a circuit.
- The builder and century are belief only. Wikipedia has no page for the fort that was read.

## Missing evidence for T2
- A survey or plan with the five bastion positions and the lost circuit (Maharashtra State Archaeology or the Nagpur District Gazetteer), and a dated source for the builder.
