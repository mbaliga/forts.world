# Chapora mapped-perimeter correction

The earlier scene used a generic oval, evenly spaced bastions, a guessed garrison building, two invented tunnel passages and four guessed tombstones. Goa Government sources describe two surviving tunnel mouths and tombstones on the southern slope, but do not establish the positions, shapes or courses invented in that scene. Those objects were removed. The existing scene for 1730 was also withdrawn: the available current outline does not establish the fort's individual buildings or their condition in that year.

The current research scene uses the outer boundary of [OpenStreetMap relation 15959326, version 3](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/15959326), whose outer member ways were joined and projected in metres east and north of 15.6059263°N, 73.7360558°E. The relation and member ways were edited on 18 October 2024; this is not the imagery date or a survey accuracy claim. © OpenStreetMap contributors, [ODbL](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). The resulting polygon is approximately 296 m east–west by 198 m north–south, with an area of approximately 3.66 ha. Its irregular form and two round plus three angular projections were visually compared with figure 25 of [Nuno Lopes, *Goa, uma perspectiva territorial de defesa (1510–1660)*](https://run.unl.pt/bitstream/10362/67520/1/rha_13-90-113.pdf), printed p. 105, an annotated Google Earth image dated 2014. The polygon is a mapped perimeter, not a measured wall-centre survey.

Lopes labels land, sea and river gates. Three openings in the model are placed approximately from their relationships in that figure; exact coordinates, profiles, accessibility and approach works require site plans and field verification. The figure marks former chapel ruins, now represented only by an approximate information point, not an invented building. The secondary gates are modeled as openings for exploration; real access may be difficult or restricted. The current fort walls, wall heights, damage, slope and ramparts remain schematic, and neither river nor shoreline is mapped into the local scene.

For terrain scale, OpenTopodata returned an [SRTM90 sample of 67 m](https://api.opentopodata.org/v1/srtm90m?locations=15.6059263%2C73.7360558) at the selected fort reference point; neighbouring samples at roughly 100–150 m offsets were 51–59 m. The current blockout uses a 67 m central plateau above its schematic water datum. A 90 m-resolution DEM cannot determine local wall foundations, gate levels or detailed relief; those need a measured surface and dated current aerial control. Retrieval date: 1 November 2026.

Next obtain the government conservation/restoration survey, current orthophoto, terrain model and dated architectural plans. Trace individual wall faces, actual openings, ramparts, chapel foundations, tunnel mouths, cemetery and approach paths. Keep the historic scene unavailable until its date-specific state is supported.

## Coarse hill surface

The generated radial hill has been replaced by 121 [SRTM30 samples via OpenTopodata](https://www.opentopodata.org/datasets/srtm/), retrieved 1 November 2026 at 80 m spacing across approximately 800 × 800 m. The sampled elevations range from 0 to 68 m and are interpolated in the scene. Gate floors now follow the approximate local surface rather than the old constant 67 m plateau. This grid cannot resolve steep cliffs, ramparts, gateway thresholds, rock-cut paths, actual sea level or any feature-level relief; it is not a topographic survey. The Chapora River, shoreline and village are not yet mapped into the scene. Existing gate positions remain approximate and the 1730 phase remains unavailable. This supersedes the older terrain-blockout description but not the other limitations.
