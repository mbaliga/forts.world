## Mapped (OpenStreetMap, ODbL)

- Site boundary way 265848074 (about 179,000 m²). OSM tags it a 60 m concrete retaining wall; that tag is **rejected** as evidence of any wall.
  The area agrees with the 180,000 m² given by two travel sources and contradicts the 18,000 m² in a third, so it is a plausible whole-property
  extent, not a wall line.
- St Anthony's church way 265848077 (about 380 m²); buildings 547314814-21 east of the fort (modern village, undated); a ruined-building footprint
  1452913909 at the west; footway 265848076 tagged `tunnel=yes` (18 m, at the eastern entrance) which marks a passage through or under the front structure.
- Footways 265848075, 557354447, 1367080301, a drain 265848073, the service road 409326108, the present coast 19852685 and 157378288.

## Inferred (flagged `inferred` in the trace)

- **Historic eras.** Boundary, coast, sea and the entrance marker are the present mapped lines carried back to 1763 and 1805 (no earlier plan found).
  The church is drawn from 1805 only, on the ground that the chapel is part of the Portuguese works; the present footprint's date is unknown.
- **Heights.** None. No source gives a height tied to a mapped element, so everything is a kerb-high plan line.

## Not drawn, although text describes it

Front wall (no mapped wall line), gate and gatehouse (only the OSM passage is marked), dry moat, bridge, bastions, tank, well, springs, barracks,

## Conflicts

- Origin: 12th century (one unsourced news page), 1679 (a lone travel site) or undated; none is used.
- Surviving fabric: Portuguese-rebuilt (Goa Government) versus pre-Portuguese (Heraldgoa, HPIP).
- British dates: three variants. Pond/tank/well/spring layout: sources disagree.
- Fort area: 18,000 m² versus 180,000 m².
