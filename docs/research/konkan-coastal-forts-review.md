# Konkan island and port forts: five-site evidence review

Review date: 31 October 2026

This batch covers Khanderi, Suvarnadurg, Vasai, Revdanda and Korlai. It links island defences, creek ports and coastal settlements. The catalogue's approximate coordinates remain unchanged. Each site has a playable level, but all five use generic or schematic geometry and remain unverified.

## Khanderi / Kanhoji Angre Island

The Directorate General of Lighthouses and Lightships documents the island's modern lighthouse, boat access from Thal and the fort's Maratha naval role. The Government of India MMR Heritage Conservation Society study includes an aerial, island context map, topographic description, well, tank, temples, tomb, bastions and an occupation chronology. Its context map is explicitly not to scale, and its printed coordinate conflicts with the DGLL lighthouse coordinate; keep the lighthouse coordinate attached to the lighthouse and do not silently use it as a fort-wall control point. The next pass should georeference a reliable island outline and surveyed fort plan, then reconstruct how fort, lighthouse, jetty and neighboring Underi relate across dated phases.

References: [DGLL: Kanhoji Angre lighthouse](https://www.dgll.nic.in/DGLL-light-house-location/about-mumbai/kanhoji-angre-lighthouse); [MMR Heritage Conservation Society, Study of Forts, Vol. 1](https://mmrhcs.org.in/images/documents/projects/precinct-studies/Revitalisation_and_Management_plan_for_the_forts_within_Mumbai_i82LQM/forts_report_vol_1.pdf); [Government of India, Khanderi site-management plan](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/270/AU2282_CuRAQ2.pdf?source=pqars).

## Suvarnadurg and Harnai

UNESCO provides the 2025 component reference and inscribed-property area; it does not give a fort-wall trace. Ratnagiri District Government places Suvarnadurg in its coastal tourism network with Kanakdurga, Harnai and nearby settlements. Maharashtra Tourism's 2024 forts leaflet describes the island, 1660 capture account and relationship to Kanakdurga. These narratives do not provide a measured outline or fix all construction dates. Next digitize a plan or measured survey, register the island, reef and shore-fort footprints, and reconcile the proposed Shilahara, Bijapur, Maratha, Angre and Peshwa phases before selecting era geometry.

References: [UNESCO component map and data](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1739/maps/); [Ratnagiri District Government tourism](https://ratnagiri.gov.in/en/tourism/); [Maharashtra Tourism forts leaflet](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/MH-Leaflet-Forts-Slides.pdf).

## Vasai / Bassein

The National Monuments Authority document references a satellite image, protected boundary and archival plan from ASI records. The Government of India MMR study includes a historical Bassein map, chronology and town context. Maharashtra Tourism describes the moat, churches, walls and bastions, though its area and wall-length figures should be checked against mapped extents. The fort was a Portuguese port city later captured by the Marathas in 1739 and occupied by the British. The next pass should digitize the archival plan and protected boundary separately, map the urban fabric and creek/sea approaches, and date each church, citadel and defensive phase.

References: [National Monuments Authority / ASI boundary and archival plan](https://www.nma.gov.in/showfile.php?lang=1&level=2&lid=2073&ls_id=1670&nma_type=0); [MMR Heritage Conservation Society, Study of Forts, Vol. 1](https://mmrhcs.org.in/images/documents/projects/precinct-studies/Revitalisation_and_Management_plan_for_the_forts_within_Mumbai_i82LQM/forts_report_vol_1.pdf); [Maharashtra Tourism: Vasai](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/fort/vasai/).

## Revdanda / Chaul

A 2024 Yojana article hosted by the Publications Division gives a Revdanda reference coordinate and describes a fort on the north bank of the Kundalika with water on three sides, a landward moat, a watchtower and multi-period remains dated between 1520 and 1721. The Maharashtra Gazetteer provides regional port and political context. These sources do not yet form a measured plan. Next identify and digitize a historical plan, align it with current shoreline and creek imagery, and separate the port settlement from the fort walls and later structures.

References: [Yojana, June 2024](https://www.publicationsdivision.nic.in/journals/Journalarchives/Yojana/Yojana-English/2024/June/Yojana%20June%2024.pdf); [Kolaba District Gazetteer, Cheul/Chaul](https://www.gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/KOLABA/places_Cheul.html).

## Korlai and the creek entrance

The Kolaba District Gazetteer describes Korlai as a narrow ridge opposite Revdanda, with a low outwork, successive cross-walls and towers; it gives an old coordinate and recounts Nizam Shahi and Portuguese activity. DGLL describes the hill, village, Revdanda backwaters, road and later lighthouse phases. A UNESCO tentative-list dossier gives broader regional coastal context but is not a protected-boundary survey. The next pass should map the long ridge and all defensive lines from a scaled plan, fit the model to terrain and current aerial, and connect the fort to Korlai village, Revdanda and the port channel.

References: [Kolaba District Gazetteer: Korlai](https://www.gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/KOLABA/places_Korlai.html); [DGLL: Korlai Fort lighthouse](https://www.dgll.nic.in/DGLL-light-house-location/about-mumbai/korlai-fort-lighthouse); [UNESCO tentative-list dossier: Konkan coastal fortifications](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6703/).

## Status

These records expand the evidence inventory; they do not make the scenes complete. The next modeling gate is a registered footprint plus terrain/shoreline, dated phases, ownership sequence, surrounding settlement and transport/supply routes. A lighthouse position, UNESCO property boundary or tourism narrative must not be substituted for a fort survey.
