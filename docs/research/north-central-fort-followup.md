# North-central fortified-city follow-up

This follow-up audits Lahore, Tughlaqabad, Fatehpur Sikri, Allahabad Fort and Jhansi. The new official sources clarify the sites and reveal better reconstruction controls, but none of these scenes is now a verified replica. Every site remains **IMPROVED GEOMETRY; NOT VERIFIED** until a measured boundary/plan is georegistered and each period is checked against dated visual evidence.

## Evidence reviewed

**Lahore Fort.** UNESCO’s 200 m-scale map identifies the fort boundary, gates, quadrangles, gardens and monument groups; its dossier also includes an aerial view. UNESCO describes the fort at the north-west corner of Lahore’s Walled City and identifies Akbar, Jahangir and Shah Jahan phases. The aerial in the dossier is documentary, not current. The playable scene still uses a generic square enclosure and one generic pavilion; it does not yet model the city, mapped quadrangles, 21 surviving monuments, or Sikh/British and current living phases.

**Tughlaqabad.** The ASI protected-monument inventory hosted by IGNCA contains site plan figure 138(a), distinguishing the eastern citadel, southern palace area, northern city, Tughlaqabad Village and the Qutb–Badarpur approach. The playable blockout hand-traces the plan outline and named settlement areas, with separate historical-city and present-village fills. The wall now follows the traced vertices directly; it is no longer replaced by a radialized ellipse-derived ring. Its scale is normalized to the written 6.5 km circuit and the outline has not been georeferenced to current imagery; gate identities, elevations, individual structures and present building footprints remain inferred or unknown. It is therefore plan-traced, not verified. Adjacent Adilabad and the broader living settlement remain missing.

**Fatehpur Sikri.** UNESCO describes a rectangular plan, right-angle road grid, six-kilometre wall, nine gates, and administrative, residential and religious quarters; the UNESCO document index lists the 1984 revenue map. The playable scene remains a generic hill enclosure. Its present-day Panch Mahal and Anup Talao cues are approximate and unplaced. No aerial comparison was completed.

**Allahabad Fort / Prayagraj.** The district government identifies Akbar’s fort at the Sangam, describes the eastern ramparts overlooking the confluence, dates the fort to 1583, and names the Ashoka Pillar and Akshayavat. It also notes limited public access. The current generated oval has no actual ramparts, confluence, city or access restrictions. It remains an exploration proxy, not a fort reconstruction.

**Jhansi Fort.** The district government describes the fort on rocky Bangra hill in the heart of Jhansi, lists ten gateways and identifies Karak Bijli tank, a garden, temple and memorials. Its pages give slightly different construction dating: one attributes a 1606–27 building range to Bir Singh Deo, another gives 1613. The scene now preserves that discrepancy, the town/fort relationship and the distinction between the Bundela construction period and 1857. None of the ten gateways has been located from a plan; the living city and Rani Mahal remain unmodeled.

## Work still required

The best immediate next step is to digitize and georeference the Lahore, Tughlaqabad and Fatehpur Sikri source maps, then compare each with a dated, appropriately licensed current aerial. This provides control geometry for walls, gates, city blocks, water edges and routes. Allahabad needs an accessible ASI plan because public entry is restricted. Jhansi needs a measured plan or a set of georeferenced official images before placing the ten gates. Each historical phase should then be rebuilt as a dated layer, with feature-level sources and an explicit measured/documented/inferred/unknown label.
