# Cabo de Rama: mapped headland pass

The old generic oval fort and arbitrary garrison building have been removed. The present scene uses [OpenStreetMap way 265848074](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/265848074) v5 as a site boundary, not a verified wall. The source is tagged as a 60 m concrete retaining wall: those attributes are not accepted as evidence of its historical material or wall height. The mapped [St Anthony's chapel](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/265848077), v3, supplies its approximate footprint only. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.

The front wall is a straight, illustrative segment following the eastern site boundary, with a provisional gate at the junction of mapped [footways](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/265848076). The mapped footway is not a surveyed gate. The scene does not reproduce the four bastions, the dry moat, actual church elevations, coast or cliff, other ruined walls, tank, well or springs. Its slope is schematic, informed only by four 90 m SRTM samples through OpenTopodata: 41, 37, 52 and 56 m. This is not terrain suitable for cliff reconstruction.

The [Goa University thesis](https://irgu.unigoa.ac.in/drs/bitstream/handle/unigoa/3898/phaldesai_p_r_2001.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1), pp. 365–369, includes fig. 7.1, an explicitly not-to-scale plan credited to the Directorate of Archives, Archaeology and Museum, Government of Goa. It describes a 15 m square tank and 5 m high, 1.60–1.75 m thick front walls. [Heritage of Portuguese Influence](https://hpip.org/en/Heritage/Details/1447) describes four cylindrical eastern bastions and retains the possibility that much surviving fabric predates Portuguese acquisition. The [Goa Government](https://www.goa.gov.in/places/cabo-de-rama-fort/) describes subsequent Portuguese rebuilding and claims the present remains are Portuguese. These accounts disagree on fabric dating; the generic 1780 scene was withdrawn pending dated plans and visual control.

The local scene anchor is 15.088, 73.921 for the coordinates above; the catalogue marker follows OSM node 2714781658 at 15.088631, 73.921643, and remains approximate. The published plan, photographs, topography and coastline must be registered before calling the reconstruction faithful.

## Shoreline pass

The local sea edge now follows part of [OpenStreetMap coastline way 19852685](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/19852685), v8 (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). The two water polygons close outside the local playable area; this is a mapped present-day shoreline, not a measured historic coastline. The water edge replaces the earlier statement that no sea is shown. The abrupt drop from the schematic plateau is only a visual approximation: exact cliffs, intertidal features and land elevations are not derived from a high-resolution terrain survey. Much of the surrounding sea and land remains outside the local scene.

## Coarse terrain interpolation

The earlier uniform east–west slope has been replaced by an inverse-distance interpolation of the four previously recorded SRTM90m samples. A 30 m smoothing radius avoids singularities, but four 90 m samples cannot resolve the fort's walls, cliff face, steps or local undulations; extrapolation beyond the samples is particularly uncertain. The coastline continues to set the water edge separately. A direct download of the archived thesis for visual registration returned HTTP 504 on this pass, so its not-to-scale plan has not been visually registered to the scene.

## Wider sampled surface and present-day paths

The four-sample interpolation is superseded by 99 [SRTM30 samples via OpenTopodata](https://www.opentopodata.org/datasets/srtm/), retrieved 1 November 2026 on a 100 m grid covering approximately 1,000 × 800 m around the scene. This coarse surface still cannot establish cliff faces, walls, steps, gateways, moat, water features or building foundations. Water remains defined separately by the present mapped coastline; the model does not contain bathymetry or historical shoreline.

The scene now paints the mapped present-day footpaths [OSM way 265848075](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/265848075) and [way 557354447](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/557354447), © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Width and surfacing are illustrative. The paths do not establish the historical routes or their period of use.

The University of Goa thesis and its not-to-scale Directorate of Archives, Archaeology and Museum map are now readable through the [university-hosted source](https://irgu.unigoa.ac.in/drs/bitstream/handle/unigoa/3898/phaldesai_p_r_2001.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1), pp. 365–369. Its descriptions and plan identify a gate, moat, tank, well, church and barracks; the map is not georeferenced, and these features have not been registered to modern imagery. The prior note about failure to retrieve the document is superseded; it remains unsuitable for directly tracing precise geometry without independent control.
