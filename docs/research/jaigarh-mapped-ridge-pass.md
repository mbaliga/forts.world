# Jaigarh mapped ridge-wall pass

The previous Jaigarh wall was a generated ellipse sized from broad 3 km × 1 km descriptions. That ellipse did not trace the fortification. It and the arbitrarily placed palace blocks and foothill houses have been removed. The 2023 government mapped structure-envelope reference and the previously documented but locally unregistered foundry and water-system cues are retained; those cues remain illustrative, not surveyed building positions.

The present-day research scene now draws separate OpenStreetMap city-wall ways 705826104, 192116414, 192283728, 192114072, 192114073, 192284729, 705826100, 192113416, 191862246, 705826154, 705826155, 705826156, 705826166, 705826167, 705826168 and 191862244. © OpenStreetMap contributors, [ODbL](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). These mapped lines are not necessarily a complete fort circuit; connections are not invented where the mapping has gaps. The rendered wall height and thickness are generic visual approximations and are not measurements. For reference see [way 705826104](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/705826104) and [way 192116414](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/192116414).

A 2.2 × 2.2 km surface of [SRTM30 samples via OpenTopodata](https://www.opentopodata.org/datasets/srtm/), retrieved 1 November 2026 at 220 m spacing, replaces the generated hill. This is coarse present-day terrain: it does not resolve wall foundations, individual ridges, gates, paths, buildings or water channels. Terrain beyond the sampled area blends toward a flat placeholder. The model uses 3 m per plan unit, 4 m per vertical unit, and a 400 m elevation reference; the displayed elevation is approximate.

The current mapping does not establish which segments existed or what they looked like in 1740, so the earlier 1740 reconstruction has been withdrawn. The relationship to Amer, the original town, cannon placement, foundry plan, reservoirs, water channels, actual gateway locations, surrounding walls and structural phases require georeferenced plans, detailed terrain, dated photographs and conservation review. A software navigation check is not evidence of historical fidelity.
