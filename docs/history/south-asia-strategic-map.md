# South Asia strategic map prototype

The game screen now has a **South Asia map** view alongside the existing Janjira tactical map. The Atlas of Empires also overlays optional South Asia terrain and modern river layers beneath its dated political map and fort timeline. Both regional views use the 44°E–100.5°E, 4.5°N–39.5°N project extent, including western Iran, the Indian subcontinent, neighbouring mainland regions, Sri Lanka, the Tibetan Plateau and the Bay of Bengal. The in-game overview's coarse coastline mask is center-sampled from the same Natural Earth regional polygons used by the atlas; its western country detail remains less complete than the India-centered core.

## How it is built

- The Godot overview uses a compact 156 × 112 Natural Earth land mask (2,184 bytes before base64 encoding) for regional coast and island placement; a reproducible builder regenerates it from the atlas basemap. The mask is modern coastline geography, not a dated historical shore.
- The Godot overview is drawn directly by one `Control`, without a node per terrain cell. It reads the same source-derived 0.25° elevation grid used by the strategic atlas, then adds a small deterministic variation for visual texture. The atlas can still display its procedural fallback if source elevations are absent. Natural Earth rivers remain a separate, modern hydrography layer.
- Clicking the map selects a named region. Hollow markers show the 130 located records in the historical-world catalogue (130/150 total sites; 130/138 forts). Clicking a marker displays its confidence and coordinate note; all currently located points are marked inferred, and 20 catalogue sites have no coordinates. A separate, off-by-default overlay can plot the 3,863 Smith–Newton (2024) supplementary rows; 3,697 rows have coordinates. Those points are source records, may repeat places, and are not a complete or verified fort inventory. Clicking one displays its source row and citation. A search field matches name, country, region or row-level source; repeated searches cycle through matches so duplicate names remain distinguishable by row number. Region selection remains a strategic placeholder for travel and campaign turns; it does not issue tactical movement orders.
- An optional Delhi–Lahore–Kabul–Kandahar corridor shows a documented itinerary sequence described for a probably c.1770–80 route scroll. The date remains uncertain. Its line is straight interpolation between modern city anchors, not a georeferenced historic road; the sidebar gives the source and caveat. It currently provides historical context, not terrain-aware travel costs or movement orders.
- The Fort World atlas route planner connects located catalogue and Smith–Newton study records through a 0.25° land grid (about 28 km north–south). The active elevations come from 315 public Mapzen Terrarium tiles at zoom 7, stratified-sampled and averaged to 0.25° using the same 10-country land mask as the router. All 14,906 router land cells have source elevations; sea cells are NODATA. Tile headers identify SRTM, GMTED and ETOPO1 source families, whose native resolution and vertical references vary. Terrain classes, hillshade styling and travel rates remain gameplay abstractions. The route is not a reconstruction of historic roads or ford conditions. Authored campaign links remain a separate waypoint-based scenario network.
- A separate, source-linked cultural-heritage layer includes Taxila, Bucephala’s Jhelum vicinity, Nalanda, Bodh Gaya, Bamiyan, Jam, Bam, Bhopal’s Kamlapati Palace and water system, Indore’s Holkar palaces and memorials, and regional context for Hyderabad’s Qutb Shahi monuments. UNESCO property/component coordinates are used where published; approximate anchors and unresolved footprint limits are disclosed. These sites are not all forts and are not filtered by campaign year.
- The fort-map layer panel independently toggles curated forts, supplementary fortification records, OSM fort points, dated towns/ports/passes, cultural heritage, scenario links, Bhopal water-body points, named Natural Earth rivers, the HydroRIVERS order-5+ regional network, state/province boundaries, country labels, terrain relief, and waypoint-coverage gaps. It retains country/province filters and name search. Terrain can render as elevation bands or satellite-inspired shaded relief; both use the same modern DEM, so the second is a cartographic style rather than imagery or a period reconstruction. Campaign-year filtering applies only to campaign places with date ranges.
- HydroRIVERS v1.0 is imported from the official Asia and Europe/Middle East shapefiles, clipped to the regional map extent, simplified at 0.025°, and compactly encoded at 1e-4° coordinate precision. The runtime subset contains 51,528 unnamed reaches at Strahler order 5 or higher, represented by 103,510 simplified vertices in about 1.49 MB of JavaScript. The lower order-4 subset was 17 MB in plain JSON and was not shipped. The order-5 threshold is a performance decision; it omits smaller waterways and is not a claim about historical importance. Source regions, counts, threshold, encoding, extent, attribution and modern-data caveat are embedded in `src/data/hydrorivers.js`.
- Terrain routing now indexes HydroRIVERS reach geometry and applies a small order-scaled seasonal crossing cost as an explicit gameplay parameter. Natural Earth remains the source for named river crossings shown in route text. HydroRIVERS main-river identifiers are not river names, and neither dataset establishes historical channels, bridge/ferry locations, fordability or seasonal discharge at a particular crossing. The rendering layer is optional and initially off. Its source citation is available in the layer panel.
- The northwest and Iran evidence views now have separate “Historic settlements” and “Documented corridor strands” controls. Seventeen sourced settlement/landscape anchors include the Uttarapatha and Gandhara network, Hadda, Kapisa/Begram, Balkh, and the Merv–Neyshabur–Rey strand. The Srinagar–Gilgit proposal is shown only as a two-node approach; the Karakoram crossing is not placed. Dashed connectors preserve source-described sequences between archaeological coordinates and approximate modern anchors; they are schematic, not surveyed historical roads. The ASI Silk Road account is a 2010 UNESCO Tentative List submission, and its proposed branches remain attributed as proposals. Bala Hisar at Charsadda is identified by Pakistan’s Department of Archaeology and Museums with ancient Pushkalavati; its point marks one mound within a wider settlement landscape.
- Heritage and historic-settlement cards expose sources, period, coordinate confidence and route origin/destination controls. These records can be endpoints for the terrain least-cost model; such a route is inferred gameplay geometry. Authored campaign-network mode only works for endpoints belonging to its authored scenario graph.
- The fort catalogue currently contains 269 records in 22 groups: 217 India, 26 Pakistan, 5 Afghanistan and 7 Iran, plus records for other neighbouring countries. Sixteen new Pakistan register points, two approximate Afghanistan additions and seven UNESCO Alamut components were added in the recent inventory pass. These are still partial inventories, not historical country totals. Smith–Newton’s 3,863 rows remain a separate supplementary source and include no Afghanistan or Iran records.
- If land-only route finding fails but the same terrain model finds a mixed land/sea path, the planner reports its schematic distance, modeled days and sea distance, and tells the player how to select mixed transport. The planner does not silently change the selected movement mode.
- Terrain-model summaries round distance to 25 km and modeled travel time to five-day increments to avoid implying precision beyond the roughly 28 km routing cells. This display rounding does not change the internal path cost or journey progression.
- Terrain-route monsoon friction uses June–September, matching the seasonal choices in the planner. A route audit checks that a September route takes longer than the same March route under current gameplay rules.
- If source values are unavailable, the Iran terrain fallback separates Zagros and Alborz mountain belts, the central plateau, Caspian lowland and Khuzestan lowland as broad gameplay regions. FAO country geography describes these physiographic systems but does not supply the procedural fallback's boundaries or elevations; see the research note for those limits.
- The compact runtime grid contains 31,640 signed integer cells (14,906 elevations and 16,734 NODATA sea cells). `npm run import:mapzen-terrain` rebuilds it from public tiles and records each downloaded tile’s `X-Imagery-Sources` header in `src/data/strategic-dem-tile-sources.csv`; raw PNGs are cached under `/private/tmp/inrts-mapzen-z7`, not bundled. `npm run build:godot-dem-grid` exports the same samples as 63,280 signed 16-bit bytes for the game overview. The mixed source is useful for regional relief, not a uniform survey-quality DEM: its constituent sources differ in resolution and datum, and no source geometry is historical.
- The Janjira map remains the local tactical layer, where direct commands use the campaign simulation's local time.

## Scope and historical status

This is a geographic prototype, not a survey-grade elevation model, political-boundary dataset or period-authentic map. Atlas and game overviews use a modern composite elevation grid, generalized river linework and reproducible visual texture; none is presented as a dated historical surface. The land mask and regional extent are approximations, and labels are modern geographic groupings. Curated marker positions come from `content/historical-world/world-catalogue.json`; the catalogue records legacy coordinates as inferred and preserves the stated position caveats. Supplementary markers come from `src/data/smith-newton-2024.json` and preserve the article row, reported coordinate, and source fields. The Smith–Newton supplement and current curated fort inventory are uneven research starting points, not a complete census, especially outside India. The heritage points show how forts connect to religious, learning, water-management and trade landscapes; they do not claim to fill those country inventories. Historical campaign packs should supply dated, culture-specific map presentation, names, political claims and sources while sharing the same underlying geographic region graph. Strategic travel remains scaled and distinct from local real-time battlefield movement.

Next map-system steps are verify every settlement anchor/link against the cited reference, expand source-backed regional settlement and travel graphs, add date-aware historical river/road layers where sources support them, create campaign-specific cartographic skins, and define tactical-map transitions for local engagements. Current corridor checks confirm that all eleven adjacent strand legs connect on the strategic grid; this is a software connectivity check, not a historical route validation.

## Route connectivity audit

Run `npm run audit:routes`, `npm run audit:hydrorivers`, `npm run audit:dem`, and `npm run audit:godot-dem-grid` from the repository root. The route audit checks that located catalogue and supplementary records within routing bounds snap to a land component, then routes ten examples, including Taxila–Bamiyan, Alamut–Bost, Alamut–Taxila and Bhopal–Indore. The HydroRIVERS audit checks encoded geometry, provenance metadata, regional bounds and representative river-corridor proximity. The active DEM audit checks bilinear interpolation, NODATA behavior and ASCII-grid conversion using synthetic data. The source-grid audit verifies every exported Godot elevation value against the atlas grid and checks the 14,906-cell coverage and reported source families. These checks validate data transfer and computation, not the absolute accuracy of Mapzen elevations, route choice, historic travel time, river crossings, political access or fort-coordinate accuracy. The catalogue and supplementary dataset also contain duplicate places, and the project catalogue is not a census of every fort.

To regenerate the active terrain layer, run `npm run import:mapzen-terrain`. The lower-level importer can also accept a pre-resampled ESRI ASCII grid at the project extent from another documented DEM source. For example, a GDAL mosaic of Copernicus GLO-90 tiles can be supplied as follows:

```sh
gdalbuildvrt /tmp/south-asia.vrt /path/to/copernicus-glo90-tiles/*.tif
gdalwarp -t_srs EPSG:4326 -te 44 4.5 100.5 39.5 -tr 0.25 0.25 -r average -dstnodata -9999 -of AAIGrid /tmp/south-asia.vrt /tmp/south-asia-0.25deg.asc
npm run import:dem -- /tmp/south-asia-0.25deg.asc
```

The elevation source is [Terrain Tiles on AWS](https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/), served as [Mapzen Terrarium tiles](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/use-service.md). Tile-level source headers are retained in the provenance CSV; source data and credit requirements are described in [Tilezen’s source catalogue](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/data-sources.md) and [attribution guidance](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md). For the exact source selected in any map tile, inspect its row in the CSV. The regional grid is bundled, so gameplay does not need a network connection. Connectivity audits remain computational checks, not evidence that a historic road followed the resulting route.

To regenerate the optional modern river network, obtain and extract the official Asia and Europe/Middle East archives linked from the [HydroRIVERS product page](https://www.hydrosheds.org/products/hydrorivers), then pass each extracted shapefile directory to `npm run import:hydrorivers -- --source <asia-folder> --source <europe-middle-east-folder>`. The importer records archive URLs and SHA-256 values for the official v1.0 files used here. It clips to the map extent and defaults to Strahler order 5+; `--order N` changes that performance threshold. It does not download or bundle the original archives. Run `npm run audit:hydrorivers` after regeneration. Confirm the current [HydroSHEDS licence](https://www.hydrosheds.org/faq) before redistributing derived data.

The Godot coastline mask can be regenerated with `npm run build:landmask` and checked cell-by-cell against the current regional polygons with `npm run audit:landmask`. It is a lightweight modern geography base, not a historical shoreline; procedural relief remains separate from the coastline. The underlying Natural Earth data are public domain and distributed through the project’s `src/data/basemap.js` regional extract.
