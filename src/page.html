Historical Worlds · R22.66

Explore forts across dated historical scenes, with living garrisons, local life and regional campaigns. Reconstructions include inferred layouts; open Sources for their evidence.

Each fort is a schematic reconstruction, not a measured survey. The Sources panel in each fort lists the plans, views, photographs and reports it is drawn from.

Stairs and ramps lead up to ramparts, bastions and summits. The plan in the corner is the fort's own plan: click it to look somewhere, double-click to walk there.

Things you can do

Click a gun, an animal, a soldier, a pen, a bastion or barracks, or a settlement sign, and a menu opens beside it.

Guns
Fire one yourself, give it a crew to fire on your order or automatically, turn it, or roll it along a level rampart (never up or down stairs, 45 m at a time). Guns can be worked at Murud-Janjira in c. 1760 and 1774.
Horses
Approach slowly to tame one, then ride it; you dismount at the foot of stairs.
Dogs
Befriend one and it follows at your heel.
Flocks
Lead sheep, goats or cattle into a pen. Penned animals breed and can be milked or sheared.
Fowl and hares
Chase and catch them, carry them to a coop. A coop breeds only with a hen and a cock inside; collect the eggs.
Soldiers
Lead a squad, put one on a gun, or garrison them in a bastion, gate tower or barracks.
Sky & weather
The sun-and-cloud button sets the hour, lets time pass, and chooses the season and weather: clear, hazy, overcast, rain, storm, fog or dust, or let the season decide. You can also call up an eclipse of the sun or the moon.
Animals
Animals and people are drawn by the game itself, at their real sizes. (Earlier builds used animated models from 0 A.D.; those are share-alike licensed and are not part of forts.world.)
Battles
Where a period has a battle (the crossed swords button), soldiers fight in companies. Select a company by its standard or any of its men; click the ground to move it or an enemy company to attack. Choose line, column, square or open order; hold, charge or fall back. Muskets fire in volleys at about 80 m, bows at 120 m; companies lose heart with their losses and break when morale fails. Each figure stands for about five men.
Wild animals
Boar, wolves, peafowl and monkeys cannot simply be tamed. Click one to see what may be tried and the odds: alone or with the men nearby, by day or night, in fair weather or rain. A capture can succeed, fail, or kill the animal; a struggle can injure your men or you, and boar and wolves can kill. Peafowl and langurs are never hunted.
Farming and stores
While time passes the seasons turn, about ten calendar days to a game day. Fields are ploughed and sown in their season, grow with the rains or a well, and are harvested by the farmers working them into the granary. Click a field for its stage and expected yield, and send more hands. Tended pens give milk and eggs to the stores; the boat from the mainland lands grain. Everyone in the fort eats from the granary; the bar at the top shows how many days the grain will last.
Garrisons
Every building can hold soldiers, by its floor area and use (about 4 m² a man in barracks, 8 in a house, 10 in a palace; ruins half). Click a building to send nearby soldiers in or call them out. In a battle, men inside fire from the windows and cannot be shot at.
The bar and the console
The bar across the top shows the fort's stores, its people and days of provisions; the orb shows the sun or moon, the hour and the date (click it for Sky & weather). Select anything and the console at the foot shows its live portrait, its properties and what it can do; Learn more opens a note on its history. Alerts, with a drumbeat and a ping on the plan, tell you when your men come under attack. Sound starts with your first click.
Settlers
Where a fort-city has empty plots (Golconda in the 1880s), open them and families walk in and build.
